# Launch plan: krikit_nl

**Krikit GmbH** · launch in Netherlands · generated on 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary


Krikit will launch Krikit Adult, an insect-protein dog food, in the Netherlands, targeting sustainability-minded owners who already buy premium food. This segment has 0.38 million households with 33% stated purchase intent, about 0.125 million households. Owners of dogs with food sensitivities number about 0.113 million intent households, but vet diets compete there and Krikit lacks clinical evidence. The product is positioned as premium food from Austrian-farmed larvae with a lower carbon footprint than beef-based dry food, not as a veterinary diet. It will be sold through pet shop chains, independent pet shops, online retailers and its own online shop with subscription, plus Instagram. The campaign presents larvae as a quality ingredient: "Premium voeding, lichtere voetafdruk."

No blocking issue is open. The Dutch price and competitor prices are unknown, so the 1.5 million euro sales goal cannot yet be checked. The negotiation with a large pet shop chain is not concluded.

## 5. Strategy


**Target:** SEG2 Sustainability-minded premium buyers. Dutch dog owners who already buy premium dry food and care about sustainability. They buy in pet shop chains and online. This is about 0.38 million households, 24% of dog-owning households [17].

**Positioning.** For sustainability-minded Dutch owners who buy premium dry food, Krikit Adult is the premium dry dog food made from Austrian-farmed insect larvae. It has no chicken, beef or grain, and a lower carbon footprint than beef-based dry food according to a 2024 life cycle study commissioned by the company. It is the sustainable choice that does not ask owners to give up premium quality. It is not a veterinary diet, so it does not compete with Hill's or Royal Canin on clinical claims. Unlike the generic "sustainable" and "organic" offer of Yarrah and the Dutch insect brands, it comes from a company founded by a veterinarian and a food engineer.

**Value proposition.** A premium complete dry dog food with 30% protein, 60% of it from insect larvae. Owners lower the environmental impact of feeding their dog without compromising on quality, and the dog accepts it readily (83% of dogs in the company's Austrian feeding test ate it from day one).

**Pricing direction.** No Dutch price exists yet, and the analysis gives no price for Yarrah, Bugs for Pets, Ofrieda, Seberus or Hill's. The only Dutch price references are Royal Canin Hypoallergenic 7 kg at about 57.50-77.57 euros [6][7][8], and that is a vet diet, not the direct competitor for this segment. Recommended direction: premium-level pricing, with no discounting against supermarket or vet-diet prices. Use the Austrian prices (14.90 euros for 1.5 kg, 54.90 euros for 7 kg [9]) only as a starting hypothesis. Before fixing the Dutch price, run a shelf-price check of the insect brands and Yarrah in pet shop chains and online. Then set the price at or slightly above the insect competitors, justified by the sustainability and brand story. Offer a subscription discount to support the online subscription goal. The size of that discount is still to be decided.

**Channels:** Pet shop chains, Independent pet shops, Own online shop with subscription, Online pet retailers, Instagram

**Rationale.** SEG2 is chosen because it combines the largest premium-intent pool with the best access for the channels Krikit is building. Using the analysis figures (my arithmetic, stated intent only): SEG2 is 0.38 million households with 33% intent, about 0.125 million households [17]. SEG1 is 0.29 million with 39%, about 0.113 million [17]. SEG3 is 0.22 million with 21%, about 0.046 million [17]. SEG4 is 0.71 million but only 6%, about 0.043 million, and it is a weak fit for a premium product [17]. SEG1 shows the highest intent but is smaller. It is also the hardest to win. Competition is strongest there: vet diets from Hill's, which presents its z/d line as "clinically proven" [13], and Royal Canin [10][6]. Bugs for Pets already makes the same hypoallergenic insect claim for the same customers [3]. Krikit's suitability claim rests only on veterinary feedback with no clinical trial [9], and the vet distributor negotiation is not concluded [brief]. So the hypoallergenic or food-sensitivity claim is not used as a lead message. SEG2 buys where Krikit is going: pet shop chains hold 29% of Dutch sales and online 18% [1]. This fits the goals of 300 outlets and 4,000 online subscribers. The need for convenient repeat purchase, which Yarrah's subscription suggests [12], supports the subscription focus. The market also supports the choice: premium dry food is 31% of the Dutch market, alternative protein grew 22% a year in 2022-2024 [1], and Dutch owners are said to care about sustainability [2]. Risks and assumptions: (1) insect competitors are already sold in the Netherlands [3][4][5], so the difference has to come from brand, quality and availability. Their prices are unknown. (2) The carbon claim comes from a company-commissioned study and must be attributed to it; the comparison is with beef-based dry food only [9]. (3) The feeding-test results are company data from Austria and should be described as such, not as proof of effect. (4) The 1.5 million euro goal cannot be checked against segment size because there is no Dutch price. Even with SEG2 at about 0.125 million intent households, reaching it will depend on price and on a listing in a large chain. This is an assumption to review once the price is set. (5) Purchase intent is stated, not actual, and the "premium price" in the survey was not defined [17]. SEG1 can be addressed later, through vets, once credible evidence exists.

## 6. Campaign


**Concept.** Krikit Adult shows that a lighter footprint and premium quality belong in the same bowl. The campaign, "De premium keuze met een lichtere voetafdruk", presents insect larvae farmed in Austria as a quality ingredient. It does not present them as a sacrifice or a gimmick. The story is told with the same calm confidence in every channel. The product facts come first (30% protein, 60% of it from insect larvae, no chicken, beef or grain), followed by the company-commissioned carbon study and the founders, a veterinarian and a food engineer. The result is a sustainable choice that does not ask owners to give up premium quality.

**Tagline:** *Premium voeding, lichtere voetafdruk.*

**Content ideas**

- Instagram Reels series 'Van larve tot kom': a short, factual look at the Austrian farm, the by-product feedstock and the team of Lena Huber and Tobias Gruber, with Dutch subtitles.
- Instagram carousel explaining the carbon figure: what the 2024 life cycle study commissioned by Krikit compared (beef-based dry food only) and what it did not.
- Instagram collaboration with Dutch dog owners and small dog-lifestyle creators who show the first-bowl moment with their own dogs. Each post is labelled as a collaboration.
- Shelf-talker and floor display for pet shop chains in Dutch, showing '30% eiwit, 60% uit insectenlarven, zonder kip, rund of graan', the carbon claim with its source, and a QR code to the subscription page.
- Starter kit for independent pet shops: counter card, a short leaflet about the founders and the Austrian farm, and a talking-points sheet for staff.
- Landing page and onboarding emails for the own online shop, built around the subscription: choose bag size, choose delivery interval, pause or stop at any time.
- Enhanced product pages for online pet retailers with a hero image, the key facts, a study-source note and a short 'Wat zit erin?' explainer.
- Launch-month sampling: small trial bags offered in participating pet shops and with online orders, with a QR code that leads to the subscription page.

**Visual direction.** Clean, warm and confident premium look. This is not 'eco-crunchy' and not clinical. Palette: deep forest green and warm off-white as the base, with an accent in soft amber or honey that nods to the larvae and the Austrian origin. Imagery: natural daylight photography of real dogs at home and outdoors, close-ups of the kibble in a bowl, and honest, calm shots of the Austrian farm and the founders at work. No cartoon insects and no shock imagery. Typography: a modern sans-serif with generous white space and bold numbers for the key facts (30% eiwit, 60% insectenlarven). Packaging cues: matte bag with a large clear 'zonder kip, rund of graan' badge, bag size (1.5 kg and 7 kg) clearly visible, a small 'Gekweekt in Oostenrijk' mark, and a footnote with the source of the carbon claim. Shelf and online materials use the same colours and fact badges so the product is recognisable in every channel.
