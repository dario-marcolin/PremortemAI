# Launch plan: lumora_es

**Claridade Lda** · launch in Spain · generated on 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary


Claridade will launch Lumora, its sunrise alarm lamp, in Spain in October 2026 at 89 euros, targeting adults aged 18-64 who find it hard to wake up in winter. It is the only segment large enough for the 40,000-unit goal: 11.4 million adults with 24% purchase intent at about 90 euros, against 2.1 million shift workers (29%), 5.3 million parents (17%) and 1.2 million students (12%). Lumora is positioned as a full-featured, simple, repairable alternative priced below Philips, without medical or comparative claims. Channels: Amazon.es, a national electronics chain, department stores, its own online shop and review media. The campaign, "Amaneceres suaves", shows the 30-minute sunrise as a gentle winter-morning ritual; tagline: "Amaneceres suaves para mañanas oscuras."

No blocking issue is open. Main gaps: no retailer agreement is confirmed, putting the 600-store goal at risk; reliable Spanish prices for Philips and Lumie are missing; the budget split is undefined.

## 5. Strategy


**Target:** SEG1 Adults with difficulty waking in winter. Spanish adults aged 18-64 who find it hard to wake up on dark winter mornings (11.4 million people, 38% of adults 18-64). They are mainstream buyers who want a gentler start to the day. They are willing to pay about 90 euros if the price feels justified.

**Positioning.** For Spanish adults who struggle to get up on dark winter mornings, Lumora is the smart sunrise alarm lamp that gives a full-featured, gradual 30-minute sunrise (up to 300 lux, deep red to daylight white), a sunset mode, 8 natural sounds and FM radio. It is simple to use, with the app or with physical buttons, and it is built to last, with a 2-year warranty and replaceable parts. It is positioned as a modern, easy-to-live-with alternative to the Philips reference models, at a clearly lower price than Philips' indicative web prices. It is also a more trustworthy step up from cheap unbranded imports, and it is more complete than Lumie's entry models. No medical, efficacy or "better than Philips/Lumie" claims are made.

**Value proposition.** A gentler wake-up on dark winter mornings, with a natural sunrise light and a soft sound. The same lamp helps you wind down in the evening with sunset mode. You get the features buyers look for (300 lux, sounds, FM radio, sunset mode) at around 90 euros. It works with or without the app and is designed to be repaired, not replaced.

**Pricing direction.** Launch at the bottom of the intended range, 89 euros, and do not go to 99 euros at launch. Reasons: (1) 89 euros is already above the 74-euro market average [1]. (2) 46% of adults not interested in the product say 90 euros is too much [6]. (3) Purchase intent of 24% was measured at "about 90 euros", and intent at 99 euros is unknown [6]. Philips is priced higher on the indicative web prices (about 142 euros for the HF3520, 167 euros or more for the HF3531). These prices conflict with the document's range and are only indicative [2][13], so the "below Philips" position is an assumption. No reliable Spanish price exists for Lumie (only a weak Rise 100 figure of about 115 euros). Before fixing the final price, Claridade should compare Philips and Lumie prices and features on the Spanish market, and could test 99 euros on its own online shop. Avoid price promotions that undercut the premium over cheap imports at 25 euros and up.

**Channels:** Amazon.es, National electronics chain, Department stores, Own online shop, Product review media

**Rationale.** SEG1 is chosen because it is the only segment large enough for the 40,000-unit goal [8]. It has 11.4 million adults and a 24% purchase intent at about 90 euros [6]. That implies roughly 2.7 million interested adults (derived: 11.4M x 24%; intent is not purchase, and overlap with other groups is unknown [6]). The alternatives are weaker on size. SEG2, shift workers, has the highest intent (29%) but only 2.1 million adults, or about 0.6 million interested (derived). 40,000 units would be about 6.6% of that pool, against about 1.5% of the SEG1 pool, so SEG2 alone is too small for the goal. SEG3, parents, has 5.3 million adults and 17% intent, about 0.9 million interested (derived), which is lower in both size and interest. SEG4, students, has 1.2 million adults, 12% intent and a low-price need that does not fit an 89-99 euro product [6]. Shift workers and parents can be reached with secondary messages (flexible alarm and sunset mode, simple physical buttons), but they are not the focus. Their needs are only weakly supported [6]. On competition, Philips is the reference and is sold in all electronics chains [2][3][4], but its HF3531 has no app or WiFi control and is described as bulky [13][9]. Lumie is premium and mainly online [2]. Cheap imports have poor reviews [2]. This leaves room for a trustworthy, full-featured option near 90 euros. On channels, marketplaces were 46% of 2024 sales, electronics chains 31% and department stores 14% [1]. Claridade already ships to Spain online (about 1,900 units in 2024 without marketing) and has a 450,000-euro budget over 18 months [7]. Amazon.es and the own online shop are therefore the safest starting channels. Amazon.es also matters for the 4.3 rating goal, although no rating data is available for it. The electronics chain and department stores are essential for the 600-store goal and for the 31% and 14% channel shares. However, negotiations are ongoing and no agreement is confirmed (brief), so the store goal is a risk. Product review media are included because Spanish review articles treat Philips as the reference [3][4], and getting Lumora into those reviews is how it can enter the comparison. Claims are limited on purpose. The Portuguese home test (64% woke more easily, 71% kept using it) had no control group and was run in Portugal [5], so it should not be used as proof of effect in Spain. Medical certification is highlighted for Philips and Lumie, and Lumora has no such claim [9]. Competitor warranty and repairability are unknown, so the 2-year warranty and replaceable parts are stated as Lumora facts, without comparison. The seasonal argument (dark mornings until about 8:30) is unverified and is not used. The seasonal clock-change debate is unresolved, so the message stays on "dark winter mornings". Launching in October 2026, before winter, fits the winter-difficulty need [1]. Assumptions: the final price is 89 euros; the channel mix depends on retailer negotiations; and the marketing budget split is not yet defined.

**Content ideas**

- Amazon.es listing with A+ content: a 30-minute sunrise timeline graphic (deep red to daylight white), a feature grid (300 lux, sunset mode, 8 sounds, FM radio) and a short video of the lamp running in a dark bedroom.
- Own online shop launch landing page in Spanish with a 'sunrise simulator' animation, an app-or-buttons explainer and a repair and replaceable parts section.
- In-store demo display for the national electronics chain: a dimmed 'dark morning' bedside corner where the lamp runs its sunrise, with shelf cards listing the key features and the 2-year warranty.
- Department store gift-season display for the winter period, with a bedside set-up and a clear price tag at 89 euros, and no discount promotions.
- Review seeding kit for Spanish product review media: a lamp, a factual spec sheet and a repairability guide, with an offer of a call with the Portuguese designers.
- Short vertical videos for the Amazon.es listing and the online shop: 'A winter morning in 30 minutes', filmed from first red glow to daylight white.
- Own online shop email series for first-time visitors and buyers: set-up tips, how to use sunset mode, and how to order spare parts.
- Printed quick-start card in the box and short how-to videos that show the app and the physical buttons, for users who prefer no app.

**Visual direction.** Calm, warm and modern. Palette built on the sunrise gradient: deep red, amber and soft orange fading into daylight white, set against a dark night-blue and charcoal for the 'before dawn' scenes. Photography of real Spanish bedrooms in winter pre-dawn, with the lamp on a bedside table and light slowly filling the room; natural textures such as linen, wood and wool. Clean, rounded product shots that show the lamp's design and its physical buttons. Light, spacious typography with a gentle sans-serif and plenty of white space. Simple icons for 30 minutes, 300 lux, sunset, sounds, FM and warranty. Packaging should be minimal and sunrise-gradient accented, with a visible 2-year warranty mark and a QR code to the repair and spare parts page. No medical imagery, no before/after body or sleep visuals, no competitor references.
