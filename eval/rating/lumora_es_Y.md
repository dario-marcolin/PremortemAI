# Launch plan: lumora_es

## 1. Executive summary


Claridade Lda, a Portuguese smart-lighting company, plans to launch **Lumora**, a sunrise alarm lamp, in Spain in October 2026 at 89 to 99 euros. The goal is 40,000 units in 18 months, listings in at least 600 stores including one national electronics chain, and an Amazon Spain rating of 4.3 or higher within a year [3].

Main conclusions:

- **The market is real but the goal is ambitious.** The Spanish wake-up light market was worth 21 million euros in 2024 and grew 9% a year [4]. At the average price of 74 euros [4], that is roughly 280,000 units a year (own calculation). The target of about 26,700 units a year [3] would need roughly 8-9% of unit volume. Claridade sold only about 1,900 units in Spain in 2024, without marketing [7]. The monthly run-rate has to rise about 14 times, from roughly 160 to roughly 2,200 units (own calculation).
- **Target segment:** adults aged 18-64 who struggle to wake up in winter. This is the largest group, 11.4 million people with 24% purchase intent [5]. Shift workers (29% intent, 2.1 million people) are a secondary target [5].
- **Positioning:** a well-designed, gentle, repairable sunrise lamp with a 2-year warranty that works with or without an app [2]. It is not the cheapest option and does not compete on medical claims.
- **Price:** launch at **89 euros**, the bottom of the range. Price is the main barrier to purchase, with 46% of non-interested adults saying 90 euros is too much [5], and Lumora is already above the 74-euro market average [4].
- **Channels:** marketplaces first (46% of category sales), then electronics chains (31%) and department stores (14%) [4], supported by the own online shop.
- **Campaign:** "Despierta con luz, no con un susto." It is built around dark winter mornings and the end-of-October clock change, and uses honest, caveated proof.
- **Main risks:** no detailed comparison with Philips has been done [6]. Evidence of effectiveness comes from an uncontrolled Portuguese test [2]. The 600-store goal exceeds Claridade's current Portuguese footprint of about 420 stores [1], and only one chain and two department store groups are in negotiation [7].

## 3. Market and competitors


### Market overview

- The Spanish wake-up light market was worth **21 million euros in 2024**, growing **9% a year** from 2022 to 2024. The average price is **74 euros** [4].
- 2024 sales by channel [4]:

| Channel | Share of sales |
|---|---|
| Online marketplaces | 46% |
| Electronics chains | 31% |
| Department stores | 14% |
| Other | 9% |

- **38% of adults** report difficulty waking up in winter [4]. The core need is dark winter mornings, with sunrise only around 8:30 in the west [3].
- Lumora's intended price of 89-99 euros is 20-34% above the market average (own calculation from [2], [4]). It is therefore a premium-leaning entry, and the 89-euro end is easier to justify.
- Seasonality: the launch falls before winter [3], and the 18-month window covers two winters (2026-27 and 2027-28) (own observation).

### Main competitors

| Competitor | Positioning | Price level |
|---|---|---|
| **Philips Wake-up Light** | Market leader, sold in all electronics chains, several models [6] | 60-200 euros [6] |
| **Lumie** | British brand, premium models, mainly online [6] | Premium. No figures in the brief [6] |
| **Unbranded imports** | Marketplace products, often with poor reviews [6] | From 25 euros [6] |
| *Lumora (for reference)* | Design-led, repairable, 2-year warranty, works with or without app [2] | 89-99 euros [2] |

Philips has entry models at about 60 euros [6], below Lumora, and a range up to 200 euros. Lumora therefore sits in the middle of Philips' price span. Whether it offers better features at its price is **unverified**, because the comparison has not been done [6].

### Customer needs

- **Wake more gently and easily** on dark winter mornings [3], [4].
- **Value for money.** Price is the main reason for not buying [5].
- **Trust in quality.** Imports are cheap but often poorly reviewed [6]. A brand with a warranty and repairability can answer this [2].
- **Simplicity.** Physical buttons mean the lamp works without the app [2].
- **Better evenings too.** Sunset mode, natural sounds and FM radio extend use beyond the alarm [2].

## 4. Segments


Segments are based on the Panel Hogar 2025 survey of Spanish adults aged 18-64, about 30 million people, measuring interest in "a sunrise alarm lamp at about 90 euros" [5]. The groups **overlap**, so interested-adult figures should not be summed. Stated intent also typically overstates real purchases (own caveat).

| Segment | Adults | Share of 18-64 | Purchase intent | Interested adults (own calc.) | Main needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Adults with difficulty waking in winter** | 11.4 million [5] | 38% [5] | 24% [5] | ~2.7 million | Gentler, easier waking on dark mornings [3], [4] |
| **Shift workers** (early or rotating) | 2.1 million [5] | 7% [5] | 29% [5] | ~0.6 million | Waking before dawn, light-based wake-up and wind-down. The mechanism is plausible but unproven (own inference) |
| **Parents of children under 10** | 5.3 million [5] | 18% [5] | 17% [5] | ~0.9 million | Calmer family mornings. Likely price-sensitive (own inference) |
| **Students living away from home** | 1.2 million [5] | 4% [5] | 12% [5] | ~0.14 million | Low-cost reliable alarm. Weakest fit with the price [5] |

Cross-cutting: interest is higher among women (27%) than men (19%) [5]. The 40,000-unit goal [3] equals about 1.5% of the roughly 2.7 million interested adults in the largest segment (own calculation).

## 5. Strategy


### Target segment

- **Primary: adults aged 18-64 who have difficulty waking in winter.** They are the largest pool (11.4 million), have solid intent (24%) and match the product's core benefit [5]. Creative and media should lean slightly towards women, who show higher interest [5].
- **Secondary: shift workers.** They have the highest intent (29%) but a smaller pool [5]. Use targeted content and partnerships rather than mass media.
- **Opportunistic: parents.** Reach them through gifting and family content, but do not lead with them. Intent is 17% [5].
- **Deprioritised: students.** Intent is the lowest (12%) and the price is an obstacle [5].
- **Geographic emphasis (hypothesis to test):** weight digital spend towards western Spain, where mornings are darkest [3]. Validate against the regional split of existing online orders [7].

### Positioning

> *For adults who dread dark winter mornings, Lumora is the well-designed sunrise lamp that wakes you gently with light, built to last and easy to repair, with a 2-year warranty.*

It differentiates on:

- **Design and durability:** a repairable design with replaceable parts and a 2-year warranty [2].
- **Colour range and versatility:** deep red to daylight white, sunset mode, sounds and FM radio [2].
- **Simplicity:** works without the app [2].
- **Trust versus imports:** a known brand with warranty versus unbranded products that often have poor reviews [6].

Each differentiator must be checked against the Philips and Lumie specifications before it appears in advertising [6].

### Value proposition

"Wake up with the light of a sunrise, even when the real one comes late. A lamp for gentle mornings and calm evenings that lasts for years."

### Pricing direction

- **Recommended RRP: 89 euros**, the bottom of the planned range [2].
  - Price is the top objection, with 46% of non-interested adults saying 90 euros is too much [5].
  - It matches the Portuguese RRP, which keeps pricing simple [2].
  - It stays well below the top of Philips' range (up to 200 euros) [6].
  - It is already above the 74-euro market average [4].
- Test 99 euros only if the comparison shows clear feature superiority and unit economics require it. Avoid different prices across channels.
- Protect margins at launch by using value-adds rather than deep discounts, for example a bundle or a gift-ready pack. Hold promotions to Black Friday and Christmas.

### Channels

| Channel | Role | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon Spain / marketplaces** | Volume driver and rating-goal enabler | 46% of category sales [4]. Claridade already sells online in Spain [7]. The 4.3 rating goal [3] is tied to this channel. |
| **National electronics chain** | Credibility and reach | 31% of sales [4]. Philips is present in all chains [6]. Under negotiation [7]. |
| **Department stores** | Gifting and premium visibility | 14% of sales [4]. Two groups under negotiation [7]. |
| **Own online shop** | Margin, data, CRM, pre-launch waitlist | Already 31% of Claridade's sales (Portugal-heavy) [1]. |

The goal of 600 stores [3] should be phased by retail partner. The chain and department-store deals set the pace, and Claridade's current 420 stores in Portugal [1] show that this is a step up.

### Rationale

- The largest and most motivated group is those who struggle with winter mornings [5], and the product's main benefit answers that need directly.
- The budget of 450,000 euros is about 11 euros per target unit (own calculation from [3], [7]). That is modest against a market leader, so focus and efficiency matter more than broad awareness.
- The online and retail channel mix follows where category sales happen [4].
- The goal of roughly 2,200 units a month on average (own calculation from [3]) needs both marketplaces and physical retail. The own shop alone cannot deliver it.

### Proposed budget split (own proposal, to refine once margins are known)

| Item | Euros | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Marketplace and performance media (Amazon Ads, search, social) | 150,000 | 33% |
| Retail trade marketing (displays, demo units, staff training, co-op) | 100,000 | 22% |

| Creators, PR and seeding | 70,000 | 16% |
| Reviews programme, sampling and CRM | 30,000 | 7% |

### Timeline

1. **June-September 2026: prepare.** Spanish localisation of app, manual and packaging. Amazon listing. Waitlist. Seeding with 100-200 early users and creators. Retail listing negotiations. Name and trademark clearance.
2. **October-November 2026: launch.** Tie the launch to the clock change at the end of October (own suggestion; the brief cites daylight saving as a factor [3]). Run the Amazon and own-shop launch, in-store activation and PR.
3. **December 2026-February 2027: winter peak.** Gifting (Christmas and Reyes) and the "dark mornings" message.
4. **March 2027 onward:** shift emphasis to sunset mode, sleep and the evening routine. Prepare the second winter.

## 6. Campaign


### Concept: "Amanece en casa" ("Sunrise at home")

Winter mornings in Spain start in the dark [3]. Lumora brings the sunrise indoors. Instead of a jarring alarm, the room slowly fills with warm light over 30 minutes [2].

### Tagline

**"Despierta con luz, no con un susto."**
Alternative: *"Amanece en tu mesilla."*

### Key messages per channel

| Channel | Key message (Spanish) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon / marketplaces** | "Un amanecer de 30 minutos. Garantía de 2 años. Funciona con o sin app." | Lead images show the colour range and the 30-minute progression [2]. Answer: "Why not a 25-euro import?" [6] |
| **Electronics chains and department stores (in-store)** | "Pruébalo: del rojo profundo a la luz del día." | Demo units with a dark-box display. Staff cheat-sheet vs competitors, after the comparison is done [6]. |
| **Own online shop and CRM** | "Reserva la tuya antes del cambio de hora." | Waitlist, launch offer, repair and warranty information [2]. |
| **Social (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube)** | "Así amanece una mañana de invierno con Lumora." | Real-bedroom time-lapses, creator "first wake-up" videos |
| **PR and media** | "Una lámpara portuguesa de diseño, reparable y pensada para durar." | Founders' story: industrial designer and electronics engineer [1]. Repairability angle [2]. |
| **Shift-worker communities** | "Tu mañana no empieza cuando sale el sol. Tu luz, sí." | Neutral, no health claims. Partner with creators or communities in these professions (own idea) |

### Content ideas

1. **"30 minutos" time-lapse:** one continuous shot of a bedroom going from dark red to daylight white [2]. The hero asset for every channel.
2. **"Mañanas oscuras" series:** creators film their real winter wake-ups across western Spain, where mornings are darkest [3].
3. **"Primer despertar" UGC:** a call for real first-week testimonials. Disclose any sponsorship clearly.
4. **Repair-it-yourself videos:** show the replaceable parts [2] to build trust and differentiate from disposable imports [6].
5. **Evening ritual content:** sunset mode, natural sounds and radio [2], for the post-winter phase.
6. **Honest proof card:** "En una prueba en hogares de Claridade con 180 personas durante 4 semanas, el 64% dijo despertarse con más facilidad y el 71% siguió usando la lámpara" [2]. The footnote must say that it was an internal test in Portugal without a control group [2].
7. **Gift guide placements** for Christmas.

**Claims discipline:** avoid medical or therapeutic promises and do not present the 64% result as a proven effect. The test was self-reported, uncontrolled and Portuguese [2].

### Visual direction

- **Palette:** gradient from deep red through amber to daylight white, mirroring the lamp's colour range [2], against a dark, calm blue-grey bedroom.
- **Photography:** natural, lived-in bedrooms at dawn, with the product as the light source and no clinical or "wellness lab" look.
- **Product:** shown simply, emphasising industrial design (a founder's discipline [1]) and visible replaceable parts [2].
- **Typography and tone:** warm and friendly Spanish, with short lines and a lightly humorous contrast between a gentle sunrise and a harsh alarm.
- **Packaging and retail displays:** a dark display box with a looping sunrise so shoppers can see the effect in a bright store.
