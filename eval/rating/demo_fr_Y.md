# Launch plan: demo_fr

## 1. Executive summary


Verdo Foods plans to launch Proteo Crisps, its chickpea and pea-protein baked crisps, in France in March 2026, starting in the Paris, Lyon and Lille regions [1][3]. The product has 15 g of protein per 40 g bag, a Nutri-Score A rating on two of its three flavours, six ingredients, and vegan and gluten-free certification [2]. The French protein snack category is worth €480 million and grows about 11% a year [4].

We recommend the following:

- **Primary target:** health-conscious office workers and everyday snackers who also practise sport weekly. This group combines a large population with strong purchase intent, and it fits the supermarket channel that handles most category sales [4][5].
- **Secondary target:** weekly sport practitioners, reached mainly through gyms and sport and organic shops rather than through sport-brand positioning, where the strongest competitor already sits [5][6].
- **Positioning:** "the crunchy snack that happens to be high in protein." Proteo Crisps should be sold as a crisp that delivers protein, not as a sports supplement or a niche vegan product, in line with the company's mission [1].
- **Pricing:** premium to mainstream crisps, with the level set after a competitor price benchmark that has not yet been done [6].
- **Lead flavour:** Herbes de Provence, once ready, rather than Smoked Paprika, which a quarter of testers found too strong and which carries a Nutri-Score B [2].
- **Core campaign:** "Croquez vos protéines" ("Crunch your protein"), built around texture, taste and the Nutri-Score A on the front of pack.

Before the launch can be finalised, the team must close several open points: price, the choice of national distributor, the target group decision, the marketing budget, and the French labelling and claims review [8].

## 2. Company brief


**Company.** Verdo Foods S.r.l. is an Italian food company founded in 2020 in Bologna by Giulia Ferri (food technologist) and Marco Bassi (former retail category manager). It makes legume-based savoury snacks with high protein content, short ingredient lists and sustainable packaging [1]. In 2024 it had 28 employees and €4.2 million turnover, almost all in Italy. It sells through about 2,300 points of sale (supermarkets, organic shops, selected gyms) and its own online shop, which accounts for 9% of sales. It was profitable in 2024 and raised a second funding round in early 2025 to finance this launch [1].

**Product.** Proteo Crisps are oven-baked crisps made from chickpea flour and pea protein. They launched in Italy in 2022 and account for 61% of company turnover [2].
- 15 g protein per 40 g bag, about 30% more than the leading plant-based protein crisps in Italy [2].
- Flavours: Sea Salt, Rosemary and Smoked Paprika. A fourth flavour, Herbes de Provence, is being developed for France [2].
- Six ingredients, no palm oil, no additives; vegan and gluten-free certified [2].
- Nutri-Score A (Sea Salt, Rosemary) and B (Smoked Paprika) [2].
- Compostable packaging since 2024; 9-month shelf life, compatible with standard supermarket logistics [2].
- Italian blind taste test (420 participants, company-run, 2024): preferred to leading conventional potato crisps by 47%, tie 21%, less preferred 32%. Texture was the most appreciated attribute; a quarter found Smoked Paprika too strong [2].

**Target country.** France. It is the second-largest savoury snack market in Europe and the fastest-growing protein snack market in Southern Europe. French consumers know Nutri-Score, which was created in France [3].

**Price.** Not stated in the brief [8].

**Competitors named.** Barebells (protein bars and snacks, sport-oriented, strong in gyms and supermarkets); Gerble (French health and wellness brand with some protein products, widely available in supermarkets); supermarket private labels (entered protein snacks in 2023 at lower prices) [6].

**Stated goals** (approved by the board, June 2025) [3]:
- 2% value share of the French protein snack category within 24 months of launch.
- Listing in at least 1,500 points of sale in the first year.
- 25% of company turnover from France by end of 2027.
- Break-even on the French operation in year three.

**Timing and operations.** Launch in March 2026. Production near Bologna runs at 55% of capacity and can supply France without new investment. A national food distributor will serve supermarkets; gyms and organic shops will be served directly in the launch regions. The online shop will be translated and ship to France from launch day. Packaging will be adapted to French labelling rules, with Nutri-Score on the front [7].

**Missing information:**
- Retail price, and the price architecture for France.
- Competitor price benchmark in France (not yet done) [6].
- Marketing and launch budget.
- Choice of target group and positioning (open) [5][8].
- Name of the national distributor (two candidates in negotiation) [8].
- Herbes de Provence development timeline and French sensory test.
- Regulatory review of French labelling and nutrition/health claims.
- Methodology and sample details of the France Snack Panel 2024 survey, and the original NutriMarket Insights report [4][5].
- Competitor sales and shelf-share data, and the private-label price points.
- Whether the Italian "30% more protein" comparison holds against French competitors, which the brief does not specify [2].

## 3. Market and competitors


**Market overview.** The French savoury snack market was worth €2.9 billion in 2024. Within it, protein snacks are a €480 million category growing at about 11% a year over 2021–2024. Plant-based products make up 18% of protein snacks, and 27% of French adults bought a protein snack in the previous three months [4].

Channel split of protein snack sales: supermarkets and hypermarkets 68%, specialised sport and organic shops 17%, online 9%, gyms and vending 6% [4].

Derived figures for context (our calculations from the reported data): the 2% share target equals about €9.6 million at 2024 category size, before growth; plant-based protein snacks are roughly €86 million [3][4].

**Competitors.**

| Competitor | Positioning (per brief) | Main channels | Price level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barebells | Protein bars and snacks; sport-oriented image | Gyms, supermarkets | Not in brief; to be benchmarked [6] |
| Gerble | French health and wellness brand; some protein products | Widely available in supermarkets | Not in brief; to be benchmarked [6] |
| Supermarket private labels | Protein snacks since 2023, lower price | Supermarkets | Lower than brands, per brief; exact levels unknown [6] |
| Conventional potato crisps (reference set in taste test) | Mainstream indulgence snack | All channels | Not in brief [2] |

**Competitive reading.** Barebells holds the sport-and-gym space that our secondary target would use, so we should not compete head-on on sport imagery [6]. Gerble has the health-and-wellness territory and French retail presence; it is the closest rival for the health-conscious positioning [6]. Private labels will set the price floor in supermarkets [6]. The brief does not show any competitor offering a crisp format with 15 g protein; this is the gap Proteo Crisps can occupy, but it needs verifying in store checks [2][6].

**Customer needs** (from the brief's consumer data and product attributes):
- Protein without compromise on taste and texture. Texture was the strongest driver in testing [2][5].
- Clear, trusted health signals that are readable on shelf, such as Nutri-Score [3].
- Plant-based and clean-label credentials, without making the product feel niche [1][2].
- An everyday snack for the office, commute and sport, not only for athletes [1][5].
- Sustainability, through compostable packaging [2].

## 4. Segments


Group sizes are adults aged 18–64 in France (about 51 million). Purchase intent is from the France Snack Panel 2024 concept test [5]. The "intent-weighted" column is our rough estimate (size × purchase intent). It is not a forecast of purchases, and the groups overlap, so the figures should not be added together.

| Segment | Size (adults) | Share of adults | Purchase intent | Intent-weighted (rough) | Main needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly sport practitioners | 17.5 million | 34% | 41% | ~7.2 million | Protein for training and recovery; convenient, non-sugary snacks; gym availability; credible protein per serving [5] |
| Health-conscious office workers | 9.0 million | 18% | 38% | ~3.4 million | Better-for-you snacking at work; short ingredient lists; Nutri-Score; taste over "diet" feel [5] |
| Parents of school-age children | 7.1 million | 14% | 22% | ~1.6 million | Lunchbox and family snacking; sensible nutrition; trust in ingredients [5] |
| Vegans and strict vegetarians | 1.3 million | 2.5% | 72% | ~0.9 million | Plant-based protein; certified vegan; clean label [5] |

**Overlap.** Many sport practitioners are also office workers, so the sport and office segments should be treated as one reachable audience: "active everyday snackers" [5].

**Reading.** Vegans show the highest enthusiasm but the smallest reachable base and are already well served by specialist brands. The founders' instinct to start with vegans is understandable, and vegans are valuable as early advocates, but they cannot deliver the 1,500-store and 2% share goals on their own [3][5]. The mission to make plant-based protein an everyday snack, not a niche product, argues against leading with this group [1].

## 5. Strategy


**Target segment.**
- **Primary:** active everyday snackers, meaning health-conscious office workers and weekly sport practitioners who eat snacks during the working day. Reached mainly through supermarkets and organic shops [4][5].
- **Secondary:** dedicated sport practitioners, reached through gyms and sport-oriented outlets in launch regions, without sport-brand claims [4][6].
- **Supporting:** vegans and vegetarians, reached through organic shops and online as advocates and early adopters [5].
- **Later (not in year one):** parents. Interest is lower (22% intent) and kids' nutrition claims carry higher regulatory and reputational risk; revisit after year one [5].

**Positioning.** For French adults who snack every day and want more protein without giving up crisp texture, Proteo Crisps are the high-protein crisp that tastes like a proper crisp, with a Nutri-Score A on the front of pack. Unlike sport bars, it is an everyday snack; unlike mainstream crisps, it delivers protein [1][2][6].

**Value proposition.**
1. **Protein in a snack you'd eat anyway:** 15 g per bag [2].
2. **Real crisp texture and taste:** preferred to conventional potato crisps by 47% in the company's blind test (Italian sample, company-run; to be validated in France) [2].
3. **Trusted, readable health signal:** Nutri-Score A (Sea Salt, Rosemary), six ingredients, no additives or palm oil [2][3].
4. **Plant-based, certified, sustainable:** vegan, gluten-free, compostable pack [2].

**Pricing direction.** The brief has no price and no competitor price data, so the following is directional only [6][8]:
- Position above mainstream crisps and private labels, justified by protein content and Nutri-Score A.
- Benchmark protein per euro against Barebells and Gerble, and private-label crisps, before setting the price.
- Test two price points in the launch regions during the first months.
- Avoid pricing above sport-bar levels, which would move the product out of the everyday-snack space.

**Channels and rationale.**
- **Supermarkets and hypermarkets (68% of category sales):** core channel, via the national distributor. Priority for the 1,500-store target. Requires Nutri-Score on front and a strong shelf-impact pack [3][4][7].
- **Organic and specialised shops (17%):** early distribution and credibility, direct-served in launch regions [4][7].
- **Gyms and sport outlets (6% vending and gyms; sport shops within the 17%):** targeted presence for secondary segment, direct-served in launch regions [4][7].
- **Online (9%):** the company's own French online shop, live at launch, for trial and recurring purchase [4][7].

**Product priorities.**
- Lead with Herbes de Provence once it is ready. Smoked Paprika (Nutri-Score B, judged too strong by a quarter of testers) should not feature on the front of French packs [2].
- Sea Salt and Rosemary carry the Nutri-Score A story and should be the core French range [2].

**Launch rationale.** Starting in Paris, Lyon and Lille gives access to dense supermarket and gym networks and allows the team to learn in three regions before national roll-out, using the existing plant capacity with no new investment [3][7]. The Italian taste result and the company's profitable track record support launching now [1][2].

**Measurement (against board goals).**
- Points of sale: at least 1,500 in year one [3].
- Value share: 2% of the French protein snack category within 24 months [3].
- France share of turnover: 25% by end 2027 [3].
- Break-even in year three [3].
- Leading indicators: distributor sell-out velocity per store, repeat rate on the online shop, Nutri-Score recall in consumer tracking, and price-test results.

## 6. Campaign


**Concept.** "Protein you can crunch." The campaign shows Proteo Crisps as a real crisp for everyday moments: the desk, the commute, the aperitif, the post-workout snack. Protein is the reason to choose it; texture and flavour are the reason to keep buying it [2][5].

**Tagline.** *"Croquez vos protéines."* (English: "Crunch your protein.")

**Key messages by channel.**

- **In-store (supermarket and organic shelf):** "15 g de protéines par sachet." Nutri-Score A on front of pack. Six ingredients. Vegan, gluten-free [2][3].
- **Gyms and sport outlets:** "Le snack protéiné qui se mange tous les jours." Best as a snack before or after training, with 15 g protein per bag, not as a sport supplement [5][6].
- **Organic shops:** six ingredients, no palm oil, compostable packaging, vegan certified [2].
- **Social media (Instagram, TikTok):** texture and taste first; protein second. Use blind-test-style short videos (French version of the 2024 Italian test, if the team runs one) [2].
- **Online shop and CRM:** Herbes de Provence as the hero flavour; subscription and multipack offers. Avoid claiming health benefits beyond the labelled nutrition facts.
- **Trade and distributor sell-in:** Nutri-Score A as a shelf argument; 9-month shelf life fits standard logistics; regional launch plan with in-store activation [3][7].
- **PR:** the Italian brand story (founded 2020 in Bologna, profitable, now in France) as the "Italian craft meets French taste" narrative [1].

**Content ideas.**
1. **"Le test du croquant":** short blind-tasting films in Paris, Lyon and Lille, filmed with local people who expected a healthy snack to taste like cardboard.
2. **"Mon midi protéiné":** a series of office-lunch snack moments with real-life protein counts.
3. **"Trois pauses, trois saveurs":** one video per flavour, Herbes de Provence first.
4. **Gym partnerships:** after-class snack sampling in partner gyms in the three regions.
5. **Nutri-Score explainer (short, factual):** how to read the A on the pack, as a trust-building piece.
6. **Ingredient-list challenge:** "six ingredients, check them" on social, with the pack as the star.
7. **Founders' story:** Giulia Ferri and Marco Bassi on why they brought a legume snack to France.

**Visual direction.**
- Bright, sunny, natural French everyday settings (kitchen counter, café terrace, office desk, park), not gym-heavy imagery.
- Packaging is the hero: Nutri-Score A visible, compostable pack cues, the French Herbes de Provence colourway as the lead.
- Close-up crisp texture shots to communicate the "real crisp" promise.
- Avoid muscle-focused, "diet" or hyper-clinical imagery. Avoid language that suggests the product is a meal replacement or treatment.
- Clear, readable protein figure ("15 g") in consistent placement across all assets.

**Timeline (summary).**
- **Now to Dec 2025:** close open points (price, target group, distributor, budget, labelling review); finish Herbes de Provence development and sensory test in France [8].
- **Jan–Feb 2026:** trade launch, shelf negotiations, gym and organic partner onboarding, content production, pre-launch social teasers [7].
- **March 2026:** launch in Paris, Lyon and Lille; online shop live [3][7].
- **Mar–Dec 2026:** monthly review of distribution count, velocity and price tests; scale decisions for national roll-out in 2027 [3].
