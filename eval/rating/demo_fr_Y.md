# Launch plan: demo_fr

## 1. Executive summary


Verdo Foods plans to launch Proteo Crisps, an oven-baked chickpea and pea-protein crisp with 15 g of protein per 40 g bag, in France in March 2026. It will start in the Paris, Lyon and Lille regions [2][3]. The French protein snack category is worth 480 million euros and grew 11% per year in 2021-2024. Plant-based products are 18% of it [4].

**Recommendation:**
- **Primary target: weekly sport practitioners.** At 17.5 million adults and 41% purchase intent, they are the largest pool [5]. Multiplying size by intent gives about 7.2 million adults (own calculation from [5]), roughly 7 times the vegan group's 0.94 million.
- **Secondary target: health-conscious office workers.** There are 9.0 million of them with 38% intent, or about 3.4 million adults (own calculation from [5]).
- **Vegans as advocates, not the core target.** They are the most enthusiastic group (72% intent) but number only 1.3 million [5]. They are best used for social seeding and credibility. Targeting them first would conflict with the stated mission of making plant-based protein "an everyday snack, not a niche product" [1].
- **Positioning:** "the protein crisp that is a real crisp". It leads with texture, the most appreciated attribute in taste tests [2], and backs this with 15 g of protein and Nutri-Score A [2][3].
- **Channels:** supermarkets first, since they carry 68% of category sales [4], supported by sport and organic shops, gyms and the online shop.
- **Open risks:**
  - The price is not defined.
  - No competitor price comparison exists [6].
  - The 1,500-point-of-sale target is ambitious. Verdo reached about 2,300 in Italy after several years [1][3].

**Missing information:**

- Marketing budget.

- Launch date for the Herbes de Provence flavour.

- Evidence that the "30% more protein" comparison holds against French competitors. The document benchmarks only against the leading plant-based protein crisps in Italy [2].

**Goal sense-check (own calculations):**
- 2% of the 2024 category is about 9.6 M€ at retail value. That is about 11% of the 86 M€ plant-based sub-segment (18% of 480 M€) [4].
- If the category keeps growing 11% per year, it reaches about 730 M€ by early 2028, and 2% is about 14.6 M€.
- For France to be 25% of turnover, with Italy flat at 4.2 M€, France would need about 1.4 M€ in net sales [1][3].
- These goals are not obviously consistent with each other. The share target is retail value, while the turnover target is Verdo's own sales. They need to be reconciled in the budget.

## 3. Market and competitors


### Market overview
- The savoury snack market in France was 2.9 billion euros in 2024. The protein snack category was 480 million euros, growing +11% per year in 2021-2024 [4].
- Plant-based products are 18% of protein snacks, and 27% of French adults bought a protein snack in the last 3 months [4].
- Sales by channel [4]:
  - Supermarkets and hypermarkets: 68%
  - Sport and organic shops: 17%
  - Online: 9%
  - Gyms and vending: 6%
- Verdo sees France as the second largest savoury snack market in Europe and the fastest growing market for protein snacks in Southern Europe [3].
- French consumers know Nutri-Score, which was created in France, so Verdo sees Nutri-Score A as a shelf argument [3].

### Competitors

| Competitor | Positioning | Price level |
|---|---|---|
| Barebells | Protein bars and snacks, strong in gyms and supermarkets, sport-oriented image [6] | Not assessed (no data) |
| Gerble | French health and wellness brand, widely available in supermarkets, some protein products [6] | Not assessed (no data) |
| Supermarket private labels | Entered protein snacks in 2023 [6] | Lower than brands [6] |

A detailed price comparison has not been done [6]. It is a priority action before the price is fixed.

### Customer needs
- **Taste and texture:** texture was the most appreciated attribute in Italian blind tests. 47% preferred Proteo to the leading conventional potato crisps, 21% tied and 32% preferred the potato crisps [2].
- **Flavour moderation:** a quarter of participants found Smoked Paprika too strong [2], so this flavour needs careful handling in France.
- **Nutrition transparency:** short ingredient list, no additives and Nutri-Score A [2][3].
- **Protein as an everyday habit, not a niche habit:** this matches Verdo's mission [1].

## 5. Strategy


### Target segment
- **Primary:** weekly sport practitioners. They are the largest group, with the second highest intent and the largest intent-weighted pool [5]. They are also reachable through supermarkets, gyms and sport shops [4].
- **Secondary:** health-conscious office workers. They are the second largest pool, with high intent and strong overlap with sport practitioners [5].
- **Advocates:** vegans and strict vegetarians. They are active on social media and enthusiastic [5], so they are used for seeding, reviews and credibility, not as the sizing basis of the plan.
- **Deferred:** parents of school-age children, who have the lowest intent (22%) [5]. Revisit in year 2.

### Positioning
*For active, health-conscious adults, Proteo Crisps are the high-protein crisps that taste and crunch like real crisps, so protein becomes an everyday snack, not a sports product.*

### Value proposition
- 15 g of protein per 40 g bag [2].
- Six ingredients, no palm oil, no additives, vegan and gluten-free [2].
- Nutri-Score A (Sea Salt, Rosemary) [2].
- Compostable packaging [2].
- Crunch preferred over conventional potato crisps by 47% of Italian testers versus 32% [2], an Italian result to be re-tested in France.

### Pricing direction
- **No price is given in the document.** Direction only, pending a competitor price study [6]:
  - Position above private labels, which are cheaper by definition [6], but avoid a gap that makes the product a niche purchase.
  - Compare price per 100 g and per gram of protein against Barebells, Gerble and private labels.
  - Test 2-3 price points in the launch regions before fixing the price.
- With the plant at 55% capacity [7], volume promotions in year 1 are feasible. Margin and the break-even target in year 3 [3] must frame how deep they can go.

### Channels
1. **Supermarkets and hypermarkets via a national distributor.** They carry 68% of category sales [4] and are required to reach 1,500 points of sale [3]. Use the 9-month shelf life, which fits standard logistics [2]. Concentrate on the Paris, Lyon and Lille regions first [3].
2. **Sport and organic shops, direct.** They carry 17% of sales [4] and give credibility with the core target.
3. **Gyms and vending.** They carry 6% of sales [4] and give visibility and sampling with sport practitioners. Verdo already sells through a selection of gyms in Italy [1].
4. **Online shop.** It is 9% of the category [4] and 9% of Verdo's Italian sales [1], and will ship to France from launch [7]. Use it for bundles, subscriptions and the Herbes de Provence preview.

### Rationale
- It follows the audience with the largest reachable pool and good intent [5].
- It follows where category sales occur [4].
- It matches Verdo's mission of mainstream plant-based protein [1].
- It uses the clearest product differentiators: taste and texture, protein level and Nutri-Score [2][3].
- Vegans stay as advocates, so enthusiasm is used without limiting scale [5].

## 6. Campaign


### Concept
**"Le vrai croustillant, version protéinée."** The campaign takes the usual trade-off of "healthy snack = boring snack" and removes it. The crunch is the hero and protein is the proof. It is credible because texture was the most appreciated attribute in tasting [2].

### Tagline
**"Croustillant. Protéiné. Sans compromis."**

### Key messages per channel

| Channel | Key message |
|---|---|
| Supermarket shelf and POS | "15 g de protéines. Nutri-Score A. Six ingrédients." The Nutri-Score A on the front of the pack is an on-shelf argument [2][3][7]. |
| Gyms and sport shops | "Le snack croustillant d'après-séance." Message: protein with real crunch, with no additives [2]. |
| Organic shops | "Pois chiches, protéine de pois, rien d'autre." Short ingredient list, vegan and gluten-free, compostable packaging [2]. |
| Online shop | Discovery pack of all flavours, a subscription offer, and early access to Herbes de Provence [2][7]. |
| Social media | "Test du croustillant": challenges and blind-test content. Vegan and sport micro-influencers as advocates [5]. |
| Workplace and office | Sampling and "pause protéinée" corners for the office-worker secondary target [5]. |

### Content ideas
- **Blind-test road show** in Paris, Lyon and Lille. Show real results, and report French results instead of Italian ones once available [2].
- **"Lis l'étiquette" series.** Compare ingredient lists and Nutri-Score on the pack with those of other snacks. Only use comparisons that are verified in France, including the "30% more protein" claim, which is currently an Italian benchmark [2].
- **Herbes de Provence** as the France-first flavour. Use it for local storytelling and regional PR [2].
- **Gym partnership content:** sampling, challenge days and post-workout snack stations.
- **Compostable pack story:** short video on the packaging [2].
- **Vegan creator recipes** that use crisps as toppings and crunch, for seeding [5].

### Visual direction
- Bright, warm and appetising. Macro close-ups of the crunch and of crisps breaking, with sound-led video.
- A pack-first look with the Nutri-Score badge and "15 g de protéines" clearly visible [2][7].
- Natural materials and colours that signal short ingredients and compostable packaging, such as chickpea, herbs and kraft textures [2].
- Active, everyday people, not bodybuilders, to reflect the mainstream mission [1].
- Avoid a "sport supplement" or "vegan-only" aesthetic, in line with the positioning.

### Launch phasing and KPIs
- **Pre-launch (Dec 2025 - Feb 2026):** distributor chosen [8], price study [6], taste test in France, pack adaptation to French labelling [7].
- **Launch (March 2026):** in-store visibility, gym sampling, social seeding and online shop live [3][7].
- **Months 3-12:** extend distribution towards the 1,500-point-of-sale goal [3]. Optimise by rate of sale. Add Herbes de Provence.
- **KPIs:** points of sale listed, rate of sale per store per week, repeat purchase, value share of the protein snack category versus the 2% goal, French share of turnover versus 25% by 2027, and progress towards year-3 break-even [3].
