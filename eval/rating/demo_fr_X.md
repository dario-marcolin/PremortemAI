# Launch plan: demo_fr

**Verdo Foods S.r.l.** · launch in France · generated on 2026-10-30

## 1. Executive summary


Proteo Crisps launches in France in March 2026 in Paris, Lyon and Lille, targeting weekly sport practitioners: 17.5 million adults, with 41% purchase intent for a similar concept. Vegans show higher intent (72%) but number only 1.3 million, and office workers, a secondary audience, number 9.0 million. The positioning is a crisp with 15 g of protein per 40 g bag and six ingredients, sold through supermarkets, sport and gym retail, organic shops, online and Instagram, under the tagline "Des protéines, croustillantes et sans compromis."

No blocking issues are open. Three gaps matter most: the price is unset and no French competitor price is verified, so a shelf-price check should come first; the marketing budget is unstated; and the French distributor is not yet named, which determines supermarket reach. Market figures come from unverified company sources.

## 3. Market and competitors


The French protein snack category is worth 480 million euros in 2024 and grows about 11% per year (2021-2024) [1]. Plant-based products account for 18% of the category [1], and 27% of French adults bought a protein snack in the last three months [1]. Supermarkets and hypermarkets hold 68% of protein snack sales, specialised sport and organic shops 17%, online 9%, and gyms and vending 6% [1]. The savoury snack market overall is worth 2.9 billion euros [1]. A web source reports that hyperprotein foods sold in French supermarkets reached about 380 million euros in 2024 [2]; this covers a broader scope than snacks and is not directly comparable with the 480 million euro snack figure. ## 5. Strategy


**Target:** SEG1 Weekly sport practitioners. Weekly sport practitioners in France who want a high-protein snack that fits around training, with a secondary focus on health-conscious office workers who overlap with this group. Vegans are a loyal but small niche and are not the primary target.

**Positioning.** For active French adults who want protein without giving up a crisp, Proteo Crisps is the plant-based protein crisp with high protein per bag, a short six-ingredient list and no additives, that tastes like a real crisp. Unlike sport bars, it is a savoury snack that can be eaten anywhere, not only at the gym.

**Value proposition.** A crisp-style snack with 15 g of protein per 40 g bag from plant sources, with a short ingredient list, vegan and gluten-free certification, and a Nutri-Score A or B front label, that can be eaten around training or at the office.

**Pricing direction.** Not yet decidable: no French competitor price is verified and the product price is not set. Competitor price levels are unknown for Barebells, Gerble and the French protein crisp set; only private labels are flagged as low-priced [1]. Recommend running a shelf-price check in Paris, Lyon and Lille supermarkets and gyms before the launch price is set, and positioning at a premium to private labels, justified by the protein content, rather than at a low price. The Belgian Näno Supps price is not a French benchmark [4].

**Channels:** Supermarkets, Sport and gym retail, Organic shops, Online shop, Instagram

**Rationale.** SEG1 is the largest group: 17.5 million adults, 34% of adults aged 18-64 [1], with the highest purchase intent among the large groups, 41% for a similar concept [1]. SEG2 is smaller (9.0 million, 18%) with 38% intent [1], and SEG1 and SEG2 overlap, so SEG2 is a secondary audience reached by the same message on the Nutri-Score and short ingredient list. SEG3 is the most enthusiastic group (72% intent) but only 1.3 million adults (2.5%) [1]; at this size it cannot deliver the 2% value share or the 1,500-store goal alone, so it is kept as an early-adopter and advocacy audience rather than the target. SEG4 has the lowest intent (22%) and a child-oriented need that the current product does not address [1]. Supermarkets and hypermarkets hold 68% of protein snack sales, and sport and organic shops 17% [1], which supports the 1,500-point distribution goal and the sport-focused channels. Sport practitioners are also reachable through gyms and sport retail, which the company already serves in Italy [brief]. The sizes are from the company document, which is not independently verified [1]. Protein content (15 g per bag) is a claim based on the company's own data, and the 30% advantage over leading Italian crisps is unverified, so the French message uses the protein figure only as stated on pack and does not claim a comparison against French competitors [1]. Assumption: the 15 g per bag claim holds for the French product as sold. The French price and the marketing budget are not set; the campaign mix should be reviewed once they are known. The French distributor is still open, which affects the supermarket channel.

## 6. Campaign


**Concept.** Protein you can actually snack on. Proteo Crisps answers the active French adult who wants protein without giving up the crisp: 15 g of plant protein in a 40 g bag, six ingredients, no additives, and a crisp that is eaten anywhere, before or after training, at the desk or on the go. Unlike a sport bar, it is a savoury snack that belongs in everyday life, not only in the gym bag. The campaign shows the crisp as the natural pick for people who train, work and live actively, and lets the short ingredient list and Nutri-Score carry the trust message for office workers who overlap with this group.

**Tagline:** *Des protéines, croustillantes et sans compromis.*

**Content ideas**

- Sampling stand in Paris, Lyon and Lille supermarkets during launch month, with a shelf-price check run alongside it
- Sampling at partner gyms and running clubs in Paris, Lyon and Lille, with a tasting of the three flavours after training sessions
- Instagram series 'Ma pause du midi' featuring office workers and weekly athletes eating Proteo Crisps at work, between sessions or on the commute
- Recipe and pairing content showing Proteo Crisps as a crunchy topping for salads, bowls and yoghurt, posted on Instagram and in-store recipe cards
- Ingredient-list reveal video: six ingredients shown on pack and read aloud, paired with the Nutri-Score front label
- Online shop launch offer with sample pack and free delivery for the first orders in France, plus a pre-launch waitlist for Herbes de Provence
- Co-branded content with two or three French sport creators on Instagram, with disclosure of paid partnerships and claims limited to the factual pack information
- Organic shop in-store display with a 'compostable pack' and 'six ingredients' shelf talker

**Visual direction.** Clean, energetic and appetising. Use a fresh palette of warm cream and off-white as the base, with deep green (rosemary and chickpea tones) and a sunlit golden-ochre accent for the crisp itself. Show the product in close-up with visible crisp texture and light breaking through the chips. Imagery shows real active people in everyday French settings: a commute, a office desk, a gym bag, a park after a run. Avoid stylised bodybuilding or clinical lab imagery and avoid any framing that suggests a medical or performance effect. Packaging cues: keep the compostable pack in kraft-toned paper finish with a clear window, the Nutri-Score front label clearly visible, the protein figure ("15 g de protéines / 40 g") set large on the front, and the six-ingredient list shown on the back. Use one flavour colour code per variety (sel de mer, romarin, paprika fumé); the Herbes de Provence variant is not shown until it is confirmed. Typography: clean sans-serif, sentence case, French copy throughout.
