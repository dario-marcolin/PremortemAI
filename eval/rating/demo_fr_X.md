# Launch plan: demo_fr

**Verdo Foods S.r.l.** · launch in France · generated on 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary


Verdo Foods will launch Proteo Crisps in France, targeting adults who practise sport weekly. This is the largest segment, 17.5M adults (34% of adults aged 18-64), with 41% purchase intent. Vegans show 72% intent but number only 1.3M, too few for the volume goals. Proteo Crisps is positioned as the savoury protein crisp, with 15 g of protein per 40 g bag and six ingredients, and as a salty alternative to Barebells bars. Channels are supermarkets and hypermarkets, gyms and sport shops, organic shops, the online shop and Instagram. The campaign "Côté salé" puts the savoury crunch into the sporty routine, with the tagline "Le snack protéiné, côté salé."

No blocking issue is open. The price is not set, and competitor shelf prices are largely unknown. The marketing budget is not set. All French market and segment figures come from one document labelled fictional, with no independent confirmation.

## 3. Market and competitors


The French protein snack category was worth 480M euros in 2024 and grew 11% per year from 2021 to 2024; plant-based products are 18% of it, and 27% of French adults bought a protein snack in the last three months [1]. It sits inside a 2.9 billion euro savoury snack market [1]. Supermarkets and hypermarkets make up 68% of protein snack sales, specialised sport and organic shops 17%, online 9%, and gyms and vending 6% [1]. The company describes France as the second largest savoury snack market in Europe and says French shoppers know Nutri-Score [2]. Trade press reports that protein-enriched products on shelves grew 86% between 2021 and 2025 [3]. Private labels are 35.7% of food sales value and are priced 30-35% below equivalent national brands on average [4]. That is a general food figure, not specific to protein snacks. ## 5. Strategy


**Target:** SEG2 Weekly sport practitioners. Adults aged 18-64 who practise sport weekly in France and buy protein snacks. They look for a high-protein snack they can find in the supermarket where they shop, and also in gyms and sport shops. Vegans and health-conscious office workers who also play sport are reached as a secondary audience through the same product.

**Positioning.** For active French adults who want protein in their snacks, Proteo Crisps is the savoury protein crisp: 15 g of protein per 40 g bag in a crunchy, oven-baked crisp with only six ingredients, no palm oil and no additives, and Nutri-Score A (Sea Salt, Rosemary). It is the salty alternative to the protein bar from Barebells, and it is a cleaner, higher-protein choice than supermarket private labels and Gerblé, whose protein claim is weak [10].

**Value proposition.** A savoury, crunchy 15 g protein snack that fits a sporty routine. It has a short, clean label that is easy to read on the shelf (six ingredients, Nutri-Score A on two flavours, vegan and gluten-free certified), and it is a salty option instead of another sweet protein bar.

**Pricing direction.** Competitor prices are largely unknown: no shelf price was found for Gerblé, private labels, Pea Pops or Le Nature, and the only Barebells prices come from online retailers and are indicative (about 2.2 euros per 55 g bar [8][9]). Recommendation: do not compete with private labels on price. They are low-priced and run 30-35% below national brands across food [4], so a price war would hurt a 15 g protein product with a clean label. Position at a premium to private labels and close to Barebells per serving. Stay inside the 15-40 euros/kg range of the weak crisp benchmark [14], and test a level under the 25 euros/kg value threshold from the same review. Before fixing the price, run a shelf-price audit of Barebells, private labels, Pea Pops and Le Nature in the target supermarkets and sport shops, and check it against Verdo's costs and the break-even goal.

**Channels:** Supermarkets and hypermarkets, Gyms and sport shops, Organic shops, Online shop, Instagram

**Rationale.** SEG2 is the best fit with the goals in the brief. Size and interest together: it is the largest segment, 17.5M adults (34% of adults aged 18-64), with 41% purchase intent [15]. SEG1 has the highest intent (72%) but is only 1.3M adults (2.5%) [15]. Even if every person in it bought, it is too small to deliver a 2% category share and 1,500 points of sale, so it cannot carry the volume goals alone. SEG3 is 9.0M with 38% intent and SEG4 is 7.1M with 22% [15]. SEG2 is therefore about twice the size of SEG3 with higher intent, and it overlaps with SEG3, so a sport-led launch also reaches part of the office-worker group. Sizes must not be added because the groups overlap, and intent was measured on a similar concept, not on the product [15]. Accessibility: supermarkets and hypermarkets are 68% of protein snack sales and sport and organic shops 17% [1], so supermarket reach through the national distributor is needed for the 1,500-point goal. Gyms and vending are only 6% [1], so gyms support credibility but cannot carry volume. This is consistent with Verdo's plan of a distributor plus direct sales to gyms and organic shops (the distributor is not yet chosen). Competition: Barebells is strong in gyms and supermarkets [5], but it is a bar, not a crisp. No Gerblé protein crisp and no private-label protein crisp were found [5][10][11]. The direct legume-crisp alternatives, Pea Pops (20% protein) [12] and Le Nature [13], have no known price or distribution data. Category: protein snacks are worth 480M euros and grow 11% a year, and 27% of adults bought one in the last three months [1]. For reference, a 2% share is about 9.6M euros at 2024 category size (my calculation); this is a company-document figure with no independent confirmation. Claims used: protein per bag, six ingredients, no palm oil, certifications and Nutri-Score, which French shoppers know [2]. Claims not used: the "30% more protein" comparison, because it was measured against the leading Italian plant-based crisp and is not valid in France, and the Italian taste test (47% preferred), because it was not run in France. Smoked Paprika was judged too strong by a quarter of Italian testers, so lead with Sea Salt and Rosemary (Nutri-Score A) and test Smoked Paprika in France. Vegans (SEG1) are served as a secondary audience on Instagram and in organic shops, since the founders see them as the most active on social media [15]; this is an assumption about reach, not evidence of sales. Risks: all French market and segment data come from one document labelled fictional, with no independent confirmation; protein-enriched shelf growth [3] is general, not specific to snacks; price and marketing budget are not set.

## 6. Campaign


**Concept.** Sweet is the default for protein snacks; Proteo Crisps flips it. The campaign, "Côté salé", puts the savoury crunch at the centre of the sporty routine: after the session, on the way home, in the gym bag. Every piece shows the same proof points: 15 g of protein per 40 g bag, six ingredients, no palm oil, no additives, and Nutri-Score A on Sea Salt and Rosemary. This expresses the positioning of the savoury protein crisp for active adults.

**Tagline:** *Le snack protéiné, côté salé.*

Nutri-Score A (Sel de mer, Romarin). Retrouvez-la au rayon snacking protéiné. |

**Content ideas**

- In-store tasting stands and branded shelf-strip displays in French supermarkets and hypermarkets during launch month, placed next to the protein snack section, with the 15 g / 6 ingredients / Nutri-Score A message
- Shelf-edge and end-of-aisle displays in supermarkets showing the two Nutri-Score A flavours (Sea Salt, Rosemary) first, with a simple 'Côté salé' header
- Gym poster and counter display kit with sample bags for partner gyms and sport shops, with a QR code linking to the online shop
- Instagram Reels series 'Côté salé': short post-workout and gym-bag moments featuring the crunch, ending on the 15 g per 40 g bag proof point
- Instagram carousel explaining the label in six ingredients, one slide per ingredient, plus the certifications (vegan, gluten-free) and compostable packaging
- Organic shop shelf talkers and tasting days highlighting the short ingredient list, no palm oil, no additives and vegan and gluten-free certifications
- Online shop discovery box with the three flavours and a launch landing page built around the 'Côté salé' idea
- Collaboration with French amateur sport and fitness creators on Instagram, who share a snack routine with Proteo Crisps (claims limited to the approved product facts)

**Visual direction.** Clean, energetic and appetising, with a savoury rather than 'sweet fitness' feel. Palette: warm chickpea beige and off-white as the base, with bold accent colours per flavour (sea blue for Sea Salt, herb green for Rosemary, deep red for Smoked Paprika) and a strong dark charcoal for typography. Photography: close-up macro shots of the crisp's crunch, natural daylight, real everyday sport moments (gym bag, locker room, park run, commute) with diverse adults aged 18-64, no body-transformation imagery. Graphic device: a bold '15 g' badge and a six-dot ingredient icon repeated on all materials, plus the Nutri-Score A logo only on Sea Salt and Rosemary. Packaging cues: matte, paper-like compostable bag with large flavour name, simple typography, visible 'Côté salé' flash, vegan and gluten-free marks, and the ingredient count on the front. Text is in French, short and factual.
