# Nordbrew Oat Cold Brew: German Launch Plan

## 1. Executive summary

Kaffeekontor Nord, a Danish specialty roaster, will launch Nordbrew Oat Cold Brew in Germany in April 2026 in Hamburg, Berlin and Rhine-Ruhr [1][3]. The product is a vegan, organic cold brew with oat drink in a 250 ml can, priced at 2.49 euros before deposit [2]. The board's goals are a 3% value share of the chilled RTD coffee category within 24 months, 3,500 points of sale in year one, and 30% of company turnover from Germany by end of 2027 [3].

**Recommendation:**
- **Target segment:** commuters who buy coffee on the go at least weekly. Roughly 11.4 million adults, with 34% purchase intent, give about 3.9 million interested people (my calculation from [5]). That is about ten times the roughly 0.38 million interested specialty enthusiasts (calculation from [5]). Commuters also buy where the category sells: petrol stations, kiosks and supermarkets [4][5].
- **Role of specialty enthusiasts:** they are a secondary, low-cost seeding audience. They have the highest intent (76%) but are only 1% of adults [5]. The Danish Instagram community is 81% enthusiasts [5], so it is a biased guide to the mass market.
- **Positioning:** "the grown-up cold brew for the commute": real 18-hour cold brew coffee, oat-smooth, with no added sugar [2], in the shelves where commuters shop.
- **Price:** hold 2.49 euros for launch, but verify it against competitor shelf prices first. No price comparison exists yet [6].
- **Campaign:** "18 Stunden Vorsprung" (18 hours head start), with the German tagline **"Kalt gebrüht. 18 Stunden Vorsprung."** The 1.2 million euro first-year budget [7] is weighted to in-store visibility, sampling at transit hubs and geo-targeted digital activity.
- **Variants:** launch Original and Vanilla together, because 22% of Danish testers found Original too bitter [2]. Validate this with a German taste test before April.

**Main risks:**
- The 3% share goal is ambitious. It equals about 18.3 million euros of retail value on the 2024 category (calculation from [3][4]).
- Distribution is not secured: one wholesaler is still being negotiated and the kiosk pilot is only under discussion [7].
- Key data is missing: German taste data, competitor prices and margins (see section 2).

## 3. Market and competitors

**Market overview**
- The German chilled RTD coffee category was worth 610 M€ in 2024 and grew about 7% per year in 2021–2024. The plant-based share is 14% [4], or about 85 M€ (calculation from [4]).
- Germany is described as Europe's largest RTD coffee market, and oat drinks as the fastest-growing plant-based drink category there [3].
- 23% of adults bought chilled RTD coffee in the last three months [4].
- Sales by channel in 2024: supermarkets and discounters 54%, petrol stations and convenience 24%, train-station and airport kiosks 13%, online and other 9% [4]. Petrol stations and supermarkets together carry 78% of sales (calculation from [4]).

**What the goal implies**
- 3% of 610 M€ is about 18.3 M€ of retail value (calculation from [3][4]).
- At 2.49 € per can that is about 7.4 million cans per year, or roughly 5–6 cans per day in each of 3,500 points of sale. This is a rough calculation that ignores VAT, deposit and category growth.
- It also equals about 21% of the current plant-based sub-category (18.3 / 85.4). Nordbrew must therefore win mainly from milk-based coffee drinkers, not only from plant-based buyers. This supports a mainstream target.
- If Danish and Swedish sales stay at the 2024 level of 9.6 M€, the 30% goal needs about 4.1 M€ of German turnover (calculation from [1][3]). Retail value and company turnover differ by trade margin and tax, so the two goals should be reconciled in a price waterfall once margins are known.

**Main competitors**

| Competitor | Positioning | Price level |
|---|---|---|
| Emmi Caffè Latte | Category leader, in most supermarkets and petrol stations. Milk-based, sweetened [6] | Not documented, to be checked |
| Starbucks chilled cups (Arla) | Strong brand, wide distribution, sweetened milk coffee [6] | Not documented, to be checked |
| Oatly Iced Coffee | Oat-based, smaller distribution, mainly organic shops and big-city supermarkets [6] | Not documented, to be checked |
| Discounter private labels | Entered the category in 2022 [6] | Lower than brands (stated, no figures) [6] |
| **Nordbrew (for reference)** | Organic, vegan, low-sweetness cold brew with oat drink [2] | 2.49 € excl. deposit [2] |

The positioning gap is a product that is oat-based, low in sweetness, organic and specialty-grade, with the wide commuter-channel distribution of the leaders. Oatly is the closest product competitor but has limited distribution. The leaders are sweetened and milk-based [6].

**Customer needs** (partly inferred from the document)
- **Low sweetness:** this was the aspect Danish testers liked most [2].
- **Convenience:** a grab-and-go can bought at transport hubs, petrol stations and supermarkets [4][5].
- **Mild taste:** 22% of Danish testers found Original too bitter [2], so a smoother option matters.
- **Dietary fit:** vegan and lactose-free [2].
- **Quality cues:** organic label and single-origin beans [2] (inference).
- **Fair price:** value for money against cheaper private labels [6] (inference).

## 5. Strategy

**Target segment: commuters in Hamburg, Berlin and Rhine-Ruhr.**
- Secondary audiences: students, as an add-on in the same cities (41% intent) [5], and specialty enthusiasts as seeders.
- Specialty enthusiasts are the "brand fans". They are reached cheaply through the online shop and specialty cafés, and they help establish credibility [5].

**Positioning:** "The grown-up cold brew for the commute." It is specialty-quality coffee, cold brewed for 18 hours, with oat drink and no added sugar, available where commuters buy [2][4]. It is positioned against sweetened milk coffees [6], not as a niche organic-shop product like Oatly [6].

**Value proposition:**
- **Taste:** real cold brew coffee that is smooth and not sweet.
- **Ingredients:** oat drink, no added sugar, vegan, lactose-free, organic [2].
- **Convenience:** a recyclable can that is easy to carry [2].
- **Credibility:** a Danish specialty roaster founded by a former barista champion [1].

Any "less sugar than…" or energy claim needs legal review and comparative data, which the document does not provide. Safe claims from the document are "ohne Zuckerzusatz", the 110 mg caffeine figure and the organic label [2].

**Pricing direction:**
- Launch at 2.49 € excl. deposit [2], a premium to private labels [6] justified by organic and specialty-grade quality.
- Before April, run a shelf-price audit in the three regions. Include competitor can sizes, since price per 100 ml is the fair comparison (Nordbrew is about 1.00 € per 100 ml, calculated from [2]).
- Do not discount permanently. Use sampling and time-limited in-store promotions for trial.
- Revisit pricing after the first quarter of sales data.

**Channels** (by priority, weighted to channel sales [4]):
1. **Petrol stations and convenience (24% of category sales) and supermarkets (54%).** These are reached through the national wholesaler under negotiation [7]. Prioritise stores near transport hubs and commuter routes in the three launch regions.
2. **Train-station kiosks (13%).** Push the pilot with the ~400-kiosk chain [7]. It is the best-fit commuter touchpoint and a high-visibility showcase, and it covers about 11% of the 3,500-point target.
3. **Online shop (part of the 9% "online and other").** Ships to Germany from launch [7]. Use it for specialty fans, bundles of both variants and community building.
4. **Specialty cafés.** Selective listings for credibility, not volume [5].

**Variants:** launch Original and Vanilla together. Vanilla can serve those who find Original too bitter (22% in Denmark [2]) and the sweetened-milk-coffee drinkers of the mainstream [6]. This must be confirmed in a German taste test, and shelf space for two SKUs per store must be negotiated. Espresso Strong follows in 2026 [2].

**Proposed budget split of 1.2 M€** [7] (consultant proposal):

| Area | Share | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Trade activation (chilled-shelf visibility, POS material, listing support) | 30% | 360 k€ |
| Sampling at transit hubs and in launch cities | 25% | 300 k€ |
| Geo-targeted digital (social, video, audio) | 20% | 240 k€ |
| Out-of-home near stations and kiosks | 15% | 180 k€ |
| Specialty seeding, online shop CRM, PR | 10% | 120 k€ |

**Rationale**
- Commuters offer a market about ten times larger than enthusiasts in interested adults, and enough scale to approach the 3% share goal [3][5].
- The channel mix matches: petrol stations, kiosks and supermarkets carry 91% of category sales [4], and commuters shop there [5].
- Enthusiasts have the highest intent but are too few and are already well served by Nordbrew's online and café reach. Their strong Danish Instagram presence reflects the community's composition, not the mass market [5].
- The founders' preference for enthusiasts is understandable, and they remain a valuable secondary audience. The sales team's commuter preference is better supported by the numbers [8].

**Key risks and next steps**
- Secure the wholesaler agreement and the kiosk pilot, since the 3,500-point goal depends on them [3][7].
- Run a German taste test and a price audit before April 2026.
- Calculate margins and reconcile the share and turnover goals.
- Confirm chilled-shelf availability and cold-chain logistics, given the refrigeration requirement [2].
- Use Denmark's sales rotation per store as a benchmark, since the 3% goal implies roughly 5–6 cans per store per day.

## 6. Campaign

**Concept: "18 Stunden Vorsprung" (18 hours head start).**
- While you slept, your coffee was already working: Nordbrew is cold brewed for 18 hours [2], so you start the day ahead.
- It sets Nordic calm against the German morning rush.
- The 18-hour brewing time is a real product fact and gives a repeatable creative device.

**Tagline (German):** **"Kalt gebrüht. 18 Stunden Vorsprung."**
- Alternatives: "Kaffee, der mitkommt." and "Kalt gebrüht. Ohne Zuckerzusatz."

**Key messages per channel**

| Channel | Key message |
|---|---|
| Chilled shelf / POS (supermarkets, petrol stations) | "Kalt gebrüht. 18 Stunden Vorsprung." Highlight oat, ohne Zuckerzusatz, Bio. Use colour-coded Original and Vanilla blockers |
| Kiosks | "Dein Vorsprung für den Weg zur Arbeit." Short, glanceable, can-focused. Possible sampling or kiosk-only launch offer |
| Out-of-home (stations, transit) | Time-themed posters with the idea of the head start, for example "Noch 18 Stunden Vorsprung. Du hast Gleis 7." |
| Social / video | Behind-the-scenes cold brew story, Nordic roasters, the contrast between a rushed commute and a calm, smooth can |
| Sampling | "Probier den Vorsprung." Taste-led, with a quick choice between Original and Vanilla |
| Online shop / specialty | Origin story (Colombia, Ethiopia), the roaster's craft, variety pack, community content for enthusiasts [2] |

**Content ideas**
- "18 Stunden" short video series: the cold brew process as a timelapse, tied to the day's schedule.
- "Gleis-Geschichten": short commuter vignettes filmed in launch-city stations.
- Origin and roaster stories with Signe Holm, former barista champion [1].
- A "Original or Vanilla?" taste challenge at sampling stands and on social media.
- Barista and café voices to build credibility with specialty fans.
- Recycling and deposit messaging ("Pfand zurück, Dose zurück") that treats the 0.25 € deposit as a normal part of the product [2].
- City-specific activations in Hamburg, Berlin and Rhine-Ruhr, with local transit hubs and creators.

**Phasing (suggested):**
1. **Pre-launch (Feb–Mar 2026):** trade activation, price audit and taste test, online and specialty seeding.
2. **Launch (April 2026):** OOH, sampling, digital burst and kiosk visibility in the three regions.
3. **Sustain (from mid-2026):** retail promotions, Espresso Strong preparation and regional expansion tied to listing progress.

**Visual direction**
- Clean Nordic minimalism with a cool palette (deep blue, off-white) and one warm coffee accent. Original and Vanilla are distinguished by colour.
- Hero shot of the can with condensation, to signal "cold" and "chilled shelf".
- A large "18" as a recurring graphic device.
- Photography of real commuter moments (platforms, trams, bikes) in natural light, with no gym or influencer clichés.
- Clear, legible packaging callouts for oat, Bio and ohne Zuckerzusatz, all subject to legal review.
- Consistent look across shelf, OOH and social, so the can is recognisable at a glance.
