# Krikit Adult: Netherlands Launch Plan (May 2026)

## 1. Executive summary

Krikit GmbH, an Austrian maker of insect-protein dog food, plans to launch Krikit Adult in the Netherlands in May 2026 [1][3]. The product is a complete dry food with 30% protein, 60% of it from black soldier fly larvae, and no chicken, beef or grain [2]. The Dutch dog food market was worth 640 million euros in 2024, and alternative-protein dog food grew 22% a year from 2022 to 2024 [4]. The board's goals are 1.5 million euros of sales in year two, 300 listings in pet shops and vets in year one, and 4,000 online subscribers by the end of year two [3]. The first-year marketing budget is 350,000 euros [7].

**Recommendation in brief**

- **Lead with sustainability-minded owners who already buy premium food.** They are the largest group with high purchase intent: 0.38 million households and 33% intent, about 125,000 intent-weighted households [5]. They buy in pet shop chains and online [5], which are the two channels Krikit can open first [7].
- **Run a smaller, parallel vet track for owners of dogs with food sensitivities.** This is the highest-intent group (39%, 0.29 million households), but it buys on vet advice [5]. Krikit has no clinical trial, only veterinary feedback [2], and it would compete with Hill's and Royal Canin vet diets [6]. The vet track should therefore build evidence and trust, not make medical claims.
- **Position Krikit as a premium, gentle-on-the-dog, light-on-the-planet food.** The Dutch price is not yet set and should be fixed only after a price benchmark. The Austrian price (9.93 euros/kg in the 1.5 kg bag, 7.84 euros/kg in the 7 kg bag) is the reference point [2].
- **Run a campaign called "Een lichtere pootafdruk" ("A lighter pawprint")** across the pet shop chain, online subscription, vets and social/PR. Sustainability claims must be stated precisely and disclosed as based on a company-commissioned study [2].
- **Major gaps remain:** no Dutch price benchmark, no Dutch consumer test, no margin data, and the chain and distributor deals are not yet closed [6][7].

## 3. Market and competitors

### Market overview

- The Dutch dog food market was worth **640 million euros in 2024**. Premium dry dog food has a **31% share** [4]. The document does not say whether this share is by value or volume, so I assume value. That would put premium dry at roughly 200 million euros (calculation from [4]).
- **Alternative-protein dog food grew 22% a year** between 2022 and 2024 [4]. It is a fast-growing niche, but its absolute size is not given.
- There are **1.6 million households with at least one dog** [4].
- Channel split (2024): supermarkets 38%, pet shop chains 29%, independent pet shops and vets 15%, online 18% [4].
  - The channels Krikit can realistically use (chains, independents and vets, online) account for **62% of the market**, about 397 million euros (calculation from [4]).
  - The 1.5 million euro goal equals about **0.23%** of the total market (calculation from [3][4]). It is ambitious for a young brand but small relative to the market.
- Krikit's home profile is specialist pet shops, vets and its own online shop (35% of sales) [1], which fits the Dutch channels it is targeting.

### Competitors

The document contains no price data [6]. The price levels below are working hypotheses to be verified, not facts.

| Competitor | Positioning | Price level (hypothesis, unverified) |
|---|---|---|
| **Yarrah** | Dutch organic pet food, sold in supermarkets and organic shops [6] | Mid to premium |
| **Hill's Prescription Diet** | Hypoallergenic veterinary food, sold through vets [6] | Premium to high |
| **Royal Canin Veterinary** | Hypoallergenic veterinary food, sold through vets [6] | Premium to high |
| **Other insect-based brands (at least 2)** | Small brands, sold online [6] | Premium |
| **Pet shop chain own premium brands** | Premium private labels of the two largest chains [6] | Mid to premium, likely the main price pressure in the chain Krikit may enter |

**Competitive implications**

- Vet diets own the "sensitive dog" need with clinical credibility Krikit lacks [2][6]. Krikit should not claim to be a medical alternative.
- Yarrah is the closest sustainability-minded competitor but is anchored in supermarkets and organic shops [6]. Krikit's difference is that it is insect-protein-led and sold in specialist channels.
- Chain private labels sit on the shelf next to Krikit, so price and a clear reason to choose it matter.
- Insect-specialist rivals are online-only, which gives Krikit an advantage if it secures physical distribution [6][7].

### Customer needs

- **Owners with sensitive dogs:** a trusted vet recommendation, a novel protein without common allergens, and proof it works [2][5].
- **Sustainability-minded premium buyers:** credible, quantified environmental benefits and a food they can feel good about [2][5].
- **Price-driven supermarket buyers:** low price, which is a poor fit for a premium product [5].
- **First-time owners:** guidance and reassurance [5].
- **All owners:** a food their dog eats readily and that is easy to reorder [2].

## 5. Strategy

### Target segment

**Primary: sustainability-minded owners who already buy premium food.**
- They have the largest intent-weighted potential [5].
- They shop in the pet shop chains and online, which are the two channels Krikit can open first [5][7].
- They are the natural source of online subscribers [3].
- They need no medical proof and can be reached with the budget available.

**Secondary: owners of dogs with food sensitivities, via vets.**
- This is the highest-intent segment [5], and it gives the product a functional reason to buy beyond sustainability.
- Reaching it requires vet advocacy, and the product has only veterinary feedback, no trial [2].
- It faces entrenched vet diets [6].
- The plan is to build credibility with a limited vet programme in year one and scale it as Dutch evidence accumulates.

**Not targeted at launch:** supermarket price buyers (6% intent [5]) and first-time owners.

### Positioning

For sustainability-minded dog owners who already buy premium food, Krikit Adult is the complete dry dog food made with insect protein that is **gentle on dogs and lighter on the planet**. It has no chicken, beef or grain [2], and the company's life cycle study found a carbon footprint about 60% lower than a beef-based dry food [2].

Positioning boundaries:
- Say "suitable for many dogs with sensitivities to common proteins" only as veterinary experience, never as a medical or hypoallergenic claim [2].
- Do not position as a vet diet or a Yarrah-style organic brand.
- Do not lean on "insect" as a novelty. Lead with the benefit to the dog and the planet, and explain the insect protein as the reason.

### Value proposition

1. **For the planet:** about 60% lower carbon footprint than a beef-based dry food, according to a 2024 life cycle study commissioned by Krikit [2]. The larvae are farmed on food industry by-products [1].
2. **For the dog:** a protein source without chicken, beef or grain, suitable for most dogs with common-protein sensitivities (veterinary feedback) [2]. 83% of dogs in the company test ate it readily from the first day [2].
3. **For the owner:** a premium complete food, easy to reorder via subscription, from a company founded by a vet and a food engineer [1].

Every number must carry its context, for example "in a company test of 120 dogs in Austria" [2].

### Pricing direction

- **Reference:** Austrian prices of 14.90 euros for 1.5 kg (9.93 euros/kg) and 54.90 euros for 7 kg (7.84 euros/kg) [2] (calculation).
- **Direction:** price as a clear premium product, at or above the premium own-brand lines of the pet shop chains and below vet diets (hypothesis to verify [6]). Avoid price competition with supermarkets [5].
- **Subscription:** offer a modest discount (5–10% as a starting hypothesis) and free delivery to build toward 4,000 subscribers [3].
- **Pack sizes:** keep 1.5 kg as a trial size and 7 kg as the value size for repeat buyers [2].
- **Before fixing the price:** complete the competitor price benchmark [6], check margin for chain and distributor, and test price acceptance with Dutch owners.

### Channels

| Channel | Role | Link to goals |
|---|---|---|
| **Pet shop chain (about 150 stores)** | Main reach channel for sustainability-minded owners [5][7] | About half of the 300 listings [3][7] |
| **Vet clinics via distributor, and independent pet shops** | Credibility and the sensitive-dog segment [5][7] | Remaining listings (about 150) to reach 300 [3] |
| **Own online shop with subscription** | Direct relationship, higher margin, data [1][7] | 4,000 subscribers by end of year two [3] |
| **Social, content and PR** | Awareness and sustainability storytelling | Drives all channels |

The 300-listing goal cannot be met with the single chain (about 150 stores) alone, so independents and vets are needed to close the gap (calculation from [3][7]). Supermarkets (38% of the market [4]) are excluded.

### Rationale

- The segment with the best mix of size, intent and channel fit is sustainability-minded premium buyers [5].
- The sensitive-dog segment has the strongest need but is gated by vets and competitors Krikit cannot yet match on evidence [2][5][6].
- Sustainability messaging rests on a documented study, but it is company-commissioned, so transparency is essential [2].
- The budget of 350,000 euros [7] is too small for a mass-market launch, so focus is required.

### Sanity check on the goals (illustrative, not from the document)

If an average household spent about 300 euros a year on Krikit (an assumption), the 1.5 million euro goal would need about 5,000 households, or about 0.3% of Dutch dog households [4]. At the same assumption, 4,000 subscribers would deliver about 1.2 million euros [3]. The goals are plausible in scale, but the spend per household, the sales definition (retail or net) and subscription churn must be confirmed.

## 6. Campaign

### Concept: "Een lichtere pootafdruk" (A lighter pawprint)

The pawprint is a familiar dog symbol, and "footprint" is the language of sustainability. The campaign uses it to link the two benefits: a small, light paw mark on the ground stands for a smaller environmental footprint, and also for a dog that feels good.

**Tagline (Dutch):** **"Krikit. Een lichtere pootafdruk."**
Alternatives: "Goed voor je hond. Licht voor de planeet." / "Slim gevoerd, licht voor de aarde."

### Key messages per channel

| Channel | Key message |
|---|---|
| **Pet shop chain (in-store)** | "Zonder kip, rund of graan. Met insecteneiwit." (Without chicken, beef or grain. With insect protein.) [2] Shelf-talker: "Ongeveer 60% lagere CO₂-voetafdruk dan rundvleesbrok*" (*according to a 2024 LCA commissioned by Krikit [2]) |
| **Online and subscription** | "Nooit meer zonder brokken. Bespaar x% met een abonnement." Easy reordering, flexible delivery, pause or cancel any time (discount to be set) |
| **Vets (via distributor)** | "Een alternatieve eiwitbron voor honden met gevoeligheid voor gangbare eiwitten" [2]. Founder-vet story [1]. Honest statement that evidence comes from veterinary feedback and a feeding test, not a clinical trial [2] |
| **Social and content** | The dog's reaction first, the planet second. "Mijn hond at het vanaf dag één" with the Austrian test (83% [2], properly labelled). Explainers on how larvae are farmed on food industry by-products [1] |
| **PR** | A Dutch launch story: Austrian vet-founded start-up brings insect protein dog food to the Netherlands. Open data on the life cycle study methodology [1][2] |

### Content ideas

1. **"De pootafdruk-test":** a visual calculator showing the footprint of a year of feeding a dog on Krikit compared with a beef-based dry food, based on the life cycle study [2]. Needs the study's per-kilo figures and legal review.
2. **Dutch "proefhap" sampling:** the 1.5 kg bag as a trial, at the chain, online and at vets. Collect owner feedback and Dutch acceptance data to replace the Austrian figures.
3. **"Van restproduct tot brok":** short video series from by-products to larvae to bag, with the founders [1].
4. **Vet-founder Q&A:** Lena Huber answers frequent questions: "Wat is insecteneiwit?", "Is het veilig voor mijn hond?", "Hoe wen ik mijn hond eraan?" (transition guide).
5. **Owner stories:** Dutch owners of dogs with sensitivities share their experiences, with a disclaimer that results vary.
6. **Ambassador dogs:** a small panel of Dutch owners and micro-influencers who feed Krikit for six weeks and report weekly, mirroring the test format [2].
7. **Subscription welcome kit:** feeding calculator, transition schedule and a small bag.

### Visual direction

- **Palette:** fresh, natural tones (leaf green, warm sand) with one bright accent colour, avoiding the heavy "organic" browns and the clinical white of vet diets [6].
- **Imagery:** happy dogs, outdoors, in Dutch settings (dunes, polders, city parks). Light, open compositions with a recurring pawprint motif.
- **Insects:** show the larvae honestly but discreetly, in clean, appetising "ingredient" imagery rather than close-up shock shots. The name Krikit suggests crickets, but the protein comes from black soldier fly larvae [1][2], so imagery and copy must not imply cricket protein.
- **Typography:** friendly and modern, readable on a shelf.
- **Claims on pack and in ads:** always show the qualifier "volgens een door Krikit in opdracht gegeven studie" for the footprint claim [2], and have all sustainability and health claims checked by legal against Dutch rules before launch.

### Phasing (relative to May 2026 launch [3])

- **T-3 to T-1 months:** finalise the chain and distributor deals [7], benchmark prices [6], Dutch packaging and claims review, recruit ambassadors, start the vet outreach, build a waitlist.
- **Launch month:** online shop live, chain listing, PR and launch event, sampling start.
- **Months 2–6:** in-store activation, subscription push, vet education events, collect Dutch feedback.
- **Months 7–12:** expand to independents and vets towards 300 listings [3], publish Dutch results, decide on the vet track scale-up.

### KPIs

- Number of listed pet shops and vets (target 300 in year one) [3].
- Online subscribers (target 4,000 by end of year two) [3] and monthly churn.
- Sales (target 1.5 million euros in year two) [3].
- Trial-to-repeat rate in Dutch sampling (compare to 76% would-buy-again in the Austrian test [2]).
- Shelf rotation in the chain and share of vet recommendations.
