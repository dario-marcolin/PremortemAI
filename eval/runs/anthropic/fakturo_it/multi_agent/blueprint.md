# Launch Blueprint: Fakturo

**Kasa Software sp. z o.o.** · launch in Italy · generated on 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary

Fakturo launches in Italy for flat-rate (forfettario) freelancers who do not yet use an invoicing app. It is the largest segment with the highest interest: 1.06M freelancers and 23% purchase intent at 9 euros per month, against 6% to 11% in the other three segments. Fakturo is positioned as the app built for flat-rate freelancers that sends e-invoices to the SdI and updates a tax estimate with every invoice. The agency's free tool has no such estimate. The main channels are professional associations and accountant referrals, supported by search, social media and onboarding emails. The campaign shows the tax estimate moving as an invoice is created, with the tagline "Ogni fattura ti mostra le tasse."

No blocking issue is open. The segment sizes and purchase intent come from unverified sources and may overlap. F24 payment, requested by 41% of beta users, is not available. The split of the 600,000 euro budget is undecided.

## 3. Market and competitors

Italy made electronic invoicing mandatory for all flat-rate (forfettario) freelancers on 1 January 2024, whatever their revenue [1, 2]. The document puts the market at 3.4M freelancers with a VAT number, of which 1.9M are in the flat-rate regime [3]. 44% of flat-rate freelancers use an invoicing app, and the average price paid is 7.20 euros per month [3]. Official data show a steady inflow of new flat-rate registrations: 242,529 in 2025, or 48.5% of new VAT numbers and up 3.9% on 2024 [4]. The market is crowded and priced low. Fatture in Cloud is the leader. Its Forfettari plan costs 4 euros per month in year 1 and 8 euros per month after that [5, 6]. The tax agency offers free tools [7], and Aruba and Fattura24 start at 1 and 4 euros per month [8]. Some competitors, such as ForfettApp and Fiscozen, already cover F24 payments [9, 10], and Fakturo does not yet [11]. Among the document's customer groups, flat-rate freelancers without an app show the highest purchase intent at 9 euros per month (23%) [12]. The Fakturo beta had 68% of users still active after 3 months and a 4.4/5 rating [11].

**Customer needs**

- Understand how much tax they will owe: 58% of flat-rate freelancers without an app say they do not understand it [12]
- Comply with mandatory e-invoicing through the SdI, since late or missing e-invoices carry penalties [2]
- Pay tax instalments by F24 from the same app: 41% of Fakturo beta users asked for this [11]
- Taxes (substitute tax and contributions) are paid by F24 with a balance and advance payments, so a tax tool needs to support this schedule [18]
- Low-cost or free tools: many non-app users rely on the tax agency's free tools or on their accountant [12]
- Prices around the market average of 7.20 euros per month [3]
- Free trial before paying: competitors offer 30-day trials [14]
- Human or accountant support: online accountant services bundle a dedicated accountant [10]

**Data gaps and assumptions**

- The market figures (3.4M, 1.9M, 44%, 7.20 euros) and the customer-group sizes and purchase intent come from fictional sources named in the document (Osservatorio DigitalWork, Freelance Barometer). They could not be verified on the web.
- The four customer groups add up to 4.3M, above the 3.4M VAT-number holders stated in the same document. The groups may overlap, or the artigiani may not be part of the 3.4M. Check the sizes before using them.
- Purchase intent is stated interest at 9 euros per month, not actual purchases. It cannot be translated directly into customer numbers.
- The beta test had only 250 users, so the retention (68%) and rating (4.4) figures are weak evidence.
- Conflict on Fatture in Cloud pricing. The document says 4 to 30 euros per month, but web sources show plans up to 51 euros per month. The 4 euro Forfettari price applies only in year 1 and renews at 8 euros per month. A Shopify blog lists Fatture in Cloud from 8 euros per month.
- Web prices come from pages dated 2026, and launch is planned for January 2027, so competitor prices may change. The Fatture in Cloud Forfettari plan has a limit of 100 documents per year, which may not suit all users [14].
- The prices for Aruba, Fattura24 and Libero SiFattura rest on a single blog comparison [8]. Qonto's data comes from its own vendor blog [15]. Both are weak sources.
- The ForfettApp price was not found. The Fiscozen flat-rate price in the snippet was unclear, so both are marked unknown. The price level of Aruba and Fattura24 is assumed low from their 'from' prices only.
- Accountant-provided software (document section 6, [13]) and other small freelance apps (5 to 12 euros per month [13]) are not named, so they are not listed as individual competitors.
- No verified web figures were found for the size or growth of the invoicing-app market, nor on professional associations or how many flat-rate freelancers use an accountant.
- The MEF data count new flat-rate registrations (233,511 in 2024; 242,529 in 2025) [4]. They are not comparable with the document's stock of 1.9M flat-rate freelancers.
- The claim that competitors cover F24 is based on the ForfettApp and Fiscozen pages only [9, 10]. It was not checked whether they offer in-app payment or only storage and submission by an accountant.
- The document's goals (12,000 customers in 24 months, cancellations below 3%) have no market benchmark in the sources [1].

## 5. Strategy

**Target:** SEG1 Flat-rate freelancers without an app. Flat-rate (forfettario) freelancers in Italy who do not yet use an invoicing app. Today they send e-invoices with the tax agency's free tools or rely on their accountant. They must issue e-invoices through the SdI, and most do not understand how much tax they will owe.

**Positioning.** For flat-rate freelancers still using free tools or their accountant, Fakturo is the invoicing app that shows how much tax you will owe, updated with every invoice. It also sends your e-invoices to the SdI. Unlike the tax agency's free tool, it gives a tax estimate. Unlike Fatture in Cloud, it is built for flat-rate freelancers and is not a broad small-business suite. Do not claim F24 payment, because the feature is not available yet.

**Value proposition.** Know your tax at every moment. Issue compliant e-invoices to the SdI and see a running tax estimate for the flat-rate regime, with receipt capture and an export for your accountant, in one app and with a 30-day free trial.

**Pricing direction.** Competitor prices are mixed. The agency tool is free. Fatture in Cloud Forfettari costs 4 euros per month in year 1 and 8 euros after that. The market average paid is 7.20 euros [3, 5]. Do not try to undercut on price. Keep 9 euros per month, because the 23% purchase intent was measured at that price [12]. Lead with the annual plan at 90 euros, which is 7.50 euros per month, close to the market average. Keep the 30-day free trial to match competitors. There is no evidence on demand at a lower price. If the team wants to test a lower launch price, run it as a limited A/B test in the first months. Re-check competitor prices before launch, because they may change by January 2027.

**Channels:** Professional associations, Accountant referrals, Search ads and SEO content, Instagram and LinkedIn, Free-trial onboarding emails

**Rationale.** SEG1 is the largest and most interested segment. It has 1.06M freelancers and 23% stated purchase intent at 9 euros per month, which is about 244,000 people. This is stated interest, not purchases [12]. The alternatives are weaker. SEG2 has 0.84M people at 9% (about 76,000) and would have to switch from competitors priced at 4 to 8 euros per month [12, 5]. SEG3 has 1.5M people but only 6% intent (about 90,000) [12]. SEG4 has 0.9M people at 11% (about 99,000) [12]. SEG1 has roughly 2.5 to 3 times the intent-weighted pool of any other segment. The goal of 12,000 customers is about 1.1% of the SEG1 size, so the target looks reachable if the intent figures hold. That is my own calculation and not a market benchmark. Competition is also weaker here. These users mostly use the free agency tool or an accountant, and that tool has no tax estimate [7]. E-invoicing has been mandatory for them since 2024 [1, 2]. 58% say they do not understand their tax [12], and Fakturo's live estimate answers that need. Because they often ask their accountant, accountant referrals and professional associations are the right channels. They also support the goal of 3 association partnerships. Risks: ForfettApp and Fiscozen appear to cover F24, but the sources do not confirm whether payment happens in the app [9, 10]. 41% of beta users asked for F24 [11], so it is a roadmap priority, and the launch must not promise it. The beta retention of 68% and rating of 4.4 come from only 250 users, so I do not use them as a headline claim. The segment sizes and intent come from unverified sources, and the four groups add up to more than the 3.4M total, so sizes may overlap. The channel mix is my assumption, because the analysis has no channel performance figures. The 600,000 euro budget split is still to be decided. Since 4% of customers cancel each month against a goal of under 3%, onboarding and the trial matter as much as acquisition.

Supporting sources: [11, 1, 3, 12, 5, 9, 10, 7, 2]

## 6. Campaign

**Concept.** Every invoice answers the question flat-rate freelancers keep asking: "How much tax will I owe?" The campaign shows the tax estimate moving the moment an invoice is created, turning a vague worry into a number on the screen. This expresses the positioning: Fakturo is the invoicing app for flat-rate freelancers that shows the tax estimate with every invoice and also sends e-invoices to the SdI.

**Tagline:** *Ogni fattura ti mostra le tasse.*

**Content ideas**

- Short screen-recorded demo video showing the tax estimate updating live as a first invoice is created, cut for Instagram Reels and LinkedIn
- Co-branded webinar 'Forfettario: capire le tasse e fatturare al SdI' with professional associations, with a 30-day free trial code for members
- One-page printed and PDF leaflet for accountants to hand to flat-rate clients, explaining the accountant export and the free trial
- SEO guide series on e-invoicing to the SdI and how the flat-rate regime tax is estimated, each ending with a free trial call to action
- Search ad campaign on flat-rate invoicing and e-invoice keywords, with landing page leading with the annual plan at 90 euros (7.50 euros per month)
- Instagram carousel 'Da fattura a stima delle tasse in 3 passaggi' covering invoice, SdI sending and running estimate
- Five-email onboarding sequence over the 30-day trial: first invoice, receipt photo capture, accountant export, reading the tax estimate, trial end and plan choice
- LinkedIn posts addressed to freelancers on the topic 'Quanto dovrò di tasse?', linking to the trial

**Visual direction.** Clean, friendly and reassuring fintech look, away from the heavy tone of tax paperwork. Main colours: deep navy for trust, a fresh green for the tax estimate figure, warm off-white backgrounds, with a bright accent colour for calls to action. Hero visual: a phone showing an invoice with a large tax estimate number that ticks up, shot over real freelancer settings (a desk, a studio, a café table) across Italian cities. Rounded sans-serif typography with large, legible numbers. Simple UI close-ups on the invoice, SdI sent status, receipt photo and accountant export. Packaging and app store cues: the Fakturo logo with the tagline, and a clear '30 giorni gratis' badge. No claims about F24 payment appear anywhere in the visuals.
