# Launch Blueprint: Fakturo

**Kasa Software sp. z o.o.** · launch in Italy · generated on 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary

Fakturo launches in Italy for flat-rate (forfettario) freelancers who do not yet use an invoicing app. They number 1.06M, with 23% purchase intent at 9 euros per month, the highest of four groups. Ordinary-regime freelancers number 1.5M but show 6%. Fakturo is positioned as low-cost invoicing that also shows the tax owed. Its single, stable price sits below the 7.20-euro market average and Fatture in Cloud's 8 euros after year one. Channels are search ads and SEO, comparison and review sites, accountants and professional associations. The campaign, "Il conto giusto", shows each invoice updating a running tax estimate. Its tagline is "Fatture in regola, tasse chiare, prezzo giusto."

No blocking issue is open. The company asked for a lower price, but intent was measured only at 9 euros and the margin impact is unchecked. Market figures come only from the company document. F24 payment is missing, and competitors also offer tax estimates.

## 3. Market and competitors

Italy has 3.4 million freelancers with a VAT number, of which 1.9 million are in the flat-rate regime (regime forfettario) [1]. 44% of flat-rate freelancers already use an invoicing app, and the average price paid is 7.20 euros per month [1]. E-invoicing via the SdI became mandatory for all flat-rate freelancers on 1 January 2024, whatever their revenue [2, 3]. New flat-rate registrations are steady: 233,511 in 2024 [4] and 242,529 in 2025, 48.5% of new VAT numbers and +3.9% on 2024 [5]. The market is crowded and price-led. Fatture in Cloud is the leader, with a flat-rate offer at 4 euros per month in the first year (48 euros + VAT), rising to 8 euros per month (96 euros + VAT) afterwards [6]. Other apps start at about 1 to 5 euros per month [7, 8, 9]. The Agenzia delle Entrate offers free tools [10]. Fiscozen sells an online accountant service from 49.90 euros per month [11]. Tax-estimate and F24 features already exist in the flat-rate niche [12, 13]. Fakturo's planned price is 9 euros per month or 90 euros per year [14].

## 5. Strategy

**Target:** SEG1 Flat-rate freelancers without an app. Flat-rate (regime forfettario) freelancers in Italy who do not yet use an invoicing app. Today they mostly use the free Agenzia delle Entrate tools or ask their accountant. Many do not understand how much tax they will owe. The segment has 1.06M freelancers.

**Positioning.** For flat-rate freelancers who still invoice with free tools or through their accountant, Fakturo is the low-cost invoicing app that also shows how much tax they will owe. It is priced below the market average and below what Fatture in Cloud charges after its first-year promotion. It has a single, stable price with no jump after the first year. It is not the cheapest option on the market: the Agenzia delle Entrate tools are free and some apps start near 1 to 5 euros per month. The pitch is therefore 'a small, fair monthly cost for compliant e-invoices plus a tax estimate updated with every invoice'. It avoids claims that the analysis does not support: it does not say Fakturo is unique on tax estimates (ForfettApp and Tasse Forfettario offer them), it does not mention F24 payment (not yet available) and it does not claim the lowest price.

**Value proposition.** Compliant e-invoices sent to the SdI and a tax estimate that updates with every invoice, so you know what you will owe, for a lower monthly price than the leader's post-promotion rate and the market average. Receipt photos and an accountant export are included. Proof, to be used carefully: in the Italian beta, 68% of 250 users were still active after 3 months and rated the app 4.4 out of 5. The sample is small and the beta was not split by tax regime.

**Pricing direction.** Lower than the planned 9 euros per month, as the feedback asks for a cheaper positioning. Competitor reference points: the average price paid by flat-rate app users is 7.20 euros per month [1]. Fatture in Cloud's flat-rate plan is 4 euros per month in year one and 8 euros afterwards [6]. FatturaElettronica APP is 4.99 euros per month [9]. Aruba starts at 1 euro + VAT for 3 months [7, 8]. Fiscozen is 49.90 euros per month [11], and the Agenzia delle Entrate tools are free [10]. Recommendation: launch below 7.20 euros per month so that the 'cheaper than average and than the leader after year one' claim holds. As an assumption to be tested, try a range of roughly 5 to 7 euros per month, with an annual plan priced at a discount to the monthly one, and keep the 30-day free trial as part of the offer, not as a channel. Do not try to beat the 4-euro promotion or the free tools on price. Intent was measured only at 9 euros [17], so there is no evidence yet on demand at a lower price. Revenue per customer will fall against the 9-euro plan, and the analysis has no cost data. The company should check that a lower price still works for its margins before fixing it. Also check whether competitor prices include VAT, because several are quoted + VAT.

**Channels:** Search ads and SEO content, Price-comparison and review sites, Accountants (commercialisti), Professional associations

**Rationale.** Feedback applied: the positioning and price are now 'cheaper': below the 7.20-euro market average [1] and below Fatture in Cloud's 8 euros after year one [6], not the cheapest on the market. I also moved the target from SEG3 to SEG1, because a price claim needs known competitor prices. Those are known for flat-rate users but not for ordinary-regime users, and it fixes the segment-size warning. Segments [17]: SEG1 has 1.06M freelancers and 23% intent at 9 euros, about 244,000 people. SEG3 has 1.5M and 6%, about 90,000. SEG4 has 0.9M and 11%, about 99,000. SEG2 has 0.84M and 9%, about 76,000. These are rough products of stated figures, not forecasts, and the groups may overlap. The goal of 12,000 paying customers is about 1.1% of SEG1 and about 5% of its stated-intent pool. In SEG3 it would be about 13% of the stated-intent pool. SEG1 has the most demand, and 58% of them say they do not understand how much tax they will owe [17]. This matches Fakturo's tax estimate and flat-rate fit [14]. Competition: SEG1 is the least served by apps, as it has no app today [17]. SEG2 would have to switch from cheaper apps [6, 8, 9]. The risk: this segment's alternatives are free tools [10], so price sensitivity is high, and the tax estimate is not unique [12, 13]. Intent was measured at 9 euros only, so a lower price may raise conversion, but this is an assumption. This choice goes against the earlier wish for the largest segment. SEG3 stays reachable later through the accountant export, but is not the target. Cancellations: the beta gives no churn figure [14], and a lower price does not by itself guarantee under 3%, so that goal is not proven. Channels: non-app users use free tools or ask their accountant [17]. Search ads and SEO content catch freelancers who look up e-invoicing and tax questions, with the message 'know your tax and invoice for a small fixed price'. Comparison and review sites carry the price message, since the web sources comparing prices are of this type [7, 8, 9]. They may carry affiliate content, so the cost should be checked. Accountants are an influence on non-users [17, 15], but accountant-provided software is also a competitor [15], so the message there is the export and the accountant stays in the workflow. Professional associations serve the goal of 3 partnerships in the first year. Two are reportedly in talks, but their size and member profile are unknown, so they are not counted on for volume. The channel reach is an assumption, as the analysis has no channel figures. Data quality: market-size and intent figures come only from the company document [1, 17]. Prices are dated 2026 and launch is planned for January 2027, so they may change.

## 6. Campaign

**Concept.** "Il conto giusto": every invoice a flat-rate freelancer sends also updates a running tax estimate, so a small, fixed monthly cost buys two things at once, compliant e-invoices and clarity on what they will owe. The campaign turns the anxious question "quanto pagherò di tasse?" into a calm, everyday habit, and presents Fakturo as the fair, honest alternative to free tools and to apps whose price jumps after year one. It expresses the positioning directly: low-cost invoicing that also shows the tax, with one stable price.

**Tagline:** *Fatture in regola, tasse chiare, prezzo giusto.*

**Content ideas**

- Google search ad set around queries such as 'fattura elettronica forfettario' and 'quante tasse pago in forfettario', all pointing to a landing page with the 30-day free trial.
- SEO guide series in Italian: 'Fattura elettronica per il regime forfettario, passo per passo' and 'Come leggere la tua stima delle tasse', each with a short product demo.
- Fakturo profile and listing pages on Italian price-comparison and software review sites, with a clear single-price panel and the beta figures (250 users, 68% active after 3 months, 4.4/5).
- Short screen-capture video: create an invoice, see the tax estimate move, in under 60 seconds, for search landing pages and review-site listings.
- One-page leaflet and email template for commercialisti to share with flat-rate clients, explaining the accountant export and the receipt photo capture.
- Co-branded webinar proposal for professional associations: 'E-invoicing and tax estimate for flat-rate freelancers', with a live demo and Q&A.
- Printable member-information sheet for professional associations, in Italian, with a QR code to the free-trial page.
- Interactive landing-page widget where a visitor enters a sample invoice amount and sees how the tax estimate view works.

**Visual direction.** Clean, calm and trustworthy, with a friendly Italian feel. Palette: deep ink blue for trust, warm off-white background, and one bright accent (a fresh green or amber) to highlight the tax estimate figure. Imagery: real freelancers (designers, photographers, consultants, tradespeople) working in small studios or cafés, shot in natural light, with a phone or laptop showing the app. Key visual: a large invoice card with a smaller live "stima tasse" counter ticking up beside it, repeated across all formats. Typography: modern rounded sans-serif, big numerals, short sentences. Packaging and UI cues: the app icon is a simple "F" on a blue square; screenshots show the invoice screen and the tax estimate screen side by side; a badge reading "30 giorni gratis" and "Prezzo fisso" on ads and landing pages. For commercialisti and associations, a more sober version of the same look: more white space, fewer photos, clear feature list.
