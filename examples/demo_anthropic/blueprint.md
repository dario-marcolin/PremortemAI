# Launch Blueprint: Proteo Crisps

**Verdo Foods S.r.l.** · launch in France · generated on 2026-11-11

## 1. Executive summary

Verdo Foods will launch Proteo Crisps in France, targeting weekly sport practitioners. They are the largest group: 17.5M adults (34% of adults aged 18-64), with 41% purchase intent for a similar concept. Vegans show higher intent (72%) but number only 1.3M. Office workers (9.0M, 38%) overlap with sport practitioners, and parents have the lowest intent (22%). Proteo Crisps is positioned as a high-protein crisp that feels like a real crisp: 15 g of protein per 40 g bag, six ingredients, vegan, and Nutri-Score A on Sea Salt and Rosemary. It will be sold through supermarkets, gyms, sport and organic shops, Instagram and the own online shop. The campaign sets a real, crunchy crisp against bar-like protein snacks, with the tagline "Le vrai croustillant. Protéiné."

No blocking issues are open. The price is not set because competitor prices are unknown, so a shelf-price audit and a price test come first. The marketing budget is unknown, and the national distributor has not been chosen.

## 3. Market and competitors

France's savoury snack market was worth 2.9 billion euros in 2024, and the protein snack category 480 million euros [1]. The protein category grew 11% per year from 2021 to 2024, and plant-based products are 18% of it [1]. 27% of French adults bought a protein snack in the last three months [1]. Supermarkets and hypermarkets make up 68% of protein snack sales, specialised sport and organic shops 17%, online 9%, and gyms and vending 6% [1]. The company calls France the fastest-growing protein snack market in Southern Europe, and sees Nutri-Score, which was created in France, as a shelf argument [2]. A blog says high-protein food revenue grew more than five-fold between 2020 and 2024, with premium prices 20% to 100% above standard products. It covers all high-protein foods, not only snacks, and is a weak source [3]. The competitors named by the team are Barebells, Gerblé and private labels [4]. No reliable shelf-price comparison for protein crisps in French supermarkets is available.

**Customer needs**

- Great taste is the most important attribute of a protein snack, even among regular users; value, relevance and taste are the main barriers in protein bars [13]
- Texture was the most appreciated attribute of Proteo Crisps in Italian blind tests, while a quarter of participants found Smoked Paprika too strong, so flavour intensity matters [14]
- A clear nutrition signal on the pack: French consumers know Nutri-Score, which was created in France [2]
- Short ingredient lists and less processing: foodwatch France criticises protein products it calls ultra-processed [15]
- Fair price for the protein claim: foodwatch shows protein versions costing nearly double per kg, for example Wasa at Carrefour, 7.09 vs 13.95 euros/kg [15]
- Protein snacks that fit regular physical activity: 71% of French people exercise regularly, per a blog [3]
- Plant-based protein options: plant-based products are 18% of protein snack sales [1]
- Buying in supermarkets: they carry 68% of protein snack sales [1]

## 5. Strategy

**Target:** SEG2 Weekly sport practitioners. French adults aged 18-64 who practise sport every week and want a high-protein, tasty snack they can buy in the supermarket or at the gym, at a fair price for the protein claim.

**Positioning.** For regular sport practitioners, Proteo Crisps is the high-protein crisp that feels like a real crisp: 15 g of protein per 40 g bag, only six ingredients with no additives, vegan, and Nutri-Score A on Sea Salt and Rosemary. It is positioned as a cleaner, simpler alternative to bar-based protein snacks and to ultra-processed protein products, and as a more credible protein offer than private labels.

**Value proposition.** 15 g of plant-based protein in a crisp snack, with a short and clear ingredient list and a Nutri-Score A (Sea Salt, Rosemary), available where sport practitioners already shop.

**Pricing direction.** Competitor prices are unknown for Barebells, Gerblé and private-label protein crisps, so no exact price can be justified yet. Direction: price above private labels (described as the low-price option [4]) to carry the protein claim, but keep the premium moderate, because foodwatch shows protein versions costing nearly double per kg and fair price is a stated need [15]. The only crisp benchmark is Inlead at about 2.15 euros per 50 g bag in specialist online shops (analyst arithmetic [11]). It is rough and not a supermarket price. Before fixing the price, run a shelf-price audit in French supermarkets and gyms, and test price acceptance with the target. Use the Inlead benchmark per gram as a ceiling reference, not as a target.

**Channels:** Supermarkets, Gyms, Sport and organic shops, Instagram, Own online shop

**Rationale.** SEG2 is the best primary target because it combines the largest size with solid interest. It has 17.5M adults (34% of adults aged 18-64) and 41% purchase intent [16]. As rough arithmetic of mine (segments overlap, intent was measured on a similar concept, so this is only an order of magnitude), that is about 7M potential buyers. SEG1 (vegans) has the highest intent (72%) but only 1.3M adults, about 0.9M intenders, and plant-based is only 18% of the category [1][16]. That is too small for a 2% share and 1,500 points of sale. SEG3 (office workers, 9.0M, 38%) is half the size and overlaps with SEG2 [16]. SEG4 (parents, 7.1M) has the lowest intent, 22% [16]. It also needs mild flavours, and Smoked Paprika was judged too strong by a quarter of Italian testers [14]. Accessibility fits the company goals. Supermarkets carry 68% of protein snack sales, sport and organic shops are part of a 17% channel, and gyms and vending are 6% [1]. Verdo already sells in supermarkets, organic shops and some gyms in Italy (brief), which suits the 1,500-point goal. SEG2 needs availability in both gyms and supermarkets [16]. Competition: Barebells is sport-oriented but found only as bars, and per one Carrefour post it is exclusive there [4][6][5]. Gerblé is mostly an indirect competitor with biscuits and cereal bars [8][9]. Direct crisp comparables, such as Inlead, sit in specialist online shops [11]. Claims used are only those not flagged as unverified: 15 g protein per 40 g bag, six ingredients, vegan, Nutri-Score A for Sea Salt and Rosemary (the Nutri-Score is a shelf argument in France [2]), and short ingredients as a reply to the ultra-processed criticism [15]. I do not use the '30% more protein than the leading plant-based crisps' claim, because that comparison was made in Italy. I also do not use the Italian taste-test result, because it has not been repeated in France. Lead with Sea Salt and Rosemary, since Smoked Paprika is Nutri-Score B and was judged too strong by some testers. Herbes de Provence has not been tested. Vegan messaging stays as a secondary benefit to reach SEG1 as a bonus audience. Assumptions and risks: the sport-practitioner interest comes from a concept test, not from the product. The Nutex sample is unclear [17]. Channel shares are for the whole category, not Proteo Crisps. The distributor is not yet chosen, and a possible Barebells exclusivity at Carrefour could limit access there. The marketing budget is unknown, so the Instagram and gym activity should be scaled once it is set.

Supporting sources: [14, 2, 1, 16, 4, 6, 5, 8, 9, 11, 15, 17]

## 6. Campaign

**Concept.** Proteo Crisps is the crisp that doesn't make you choose. Built around the moment after training or in the middle of a busy week, the campaign sets a real, crunchy crisp against the "protein snack" that looks like a bar and reads like a chemistry label. We show the facts plainly: 15 g of protein, six ingredients, a Nutri-Score A on Sea Salt and Rosemary. A real crisp, with protein and nothing superfluous.

**Tagline:** *Le vrai croustillant. Protéiné.*

| Channel | Headline | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Supermarkets | 15 g de protéines. Un vrai croustillant. | Proteo Crisps : des chips au four, riches en protéines, avec 15 g de protéines par sachet de 40 g. Seulement six ingrédients, sans additifs. Nutri-Score A pour Sea Salt et Rosemary. À retrouver au rayon snacking protéiné. |
| Supermarkets | Six ingrédients. Zéro compromis sur le croquant. | Pois, pois chiche, huile, sel, et c'est tout ou presque : une liste courte et claire, sans huile de palme ni additifs. Végan, sans gluten. Sea Salt et Rosemary : Nutri-Score A. |
| Gyms | Après l'effort, du croquant. | 15 g de protéines par sachet de 40 g. Six ingrédients, sans additifs, 100 % végétal. Une chips qui a vraiment du croquant, à glisser dans votre sac de sport. Proteo Crisps : le vrai croustillant. Protéiné. |
| Sport and organic shops | Une recette courte, des protéines végétales. | Farine de pois chiche et protéine de pois, cuites au four. 15 g de protéines par sachet de 40 g, six ingrédients, sans additifs, végan et sans gluten certifiés. Emballage compostable. Essayez Sea Salt ou Rosemary. |
| Instagram | Ça, c'est une chips. Et elle a 15 g de protéines. | Pas une barre, pas une poudre : une vraie chips croustillante, riche en protéines. 6 ingrédients, Nutri-Score A (Sea Salt et Rosemary), végan. Dites-nous votre saveur préférée en commentaire. 👇 #ProteoCrisps |
| Own online shop | Goûtez le croquant protéiné. | Découvrez Sea Salt, Rosemary et Smoked Paprika : 15 g de protéines par sachet de 40 g, six ingrédients, sans additifs, végan et sans gluten. Commandez votre premier assortiment et livrez-vous à la pause croquante. |

**Content ideas**

- Supermarket shelf-ready display and shelf strips in the protein snack aisle, led by Sea Salt and Rosemary, highlighting '15 g de protéines' and Nutri-Score A
- Sampling stands in partner gyms during launch month, with sachets handed out at the reception or juice bar
- Branded gym poster and locker-room screen series: 'Après l'effort, du croquant' with the 15 g protein and six-ingredient facts
- Instagram Reels series 'Le test du croquant': crunch-focused close-ups (ASMR style) filmed in gyms and at home after training, with the ingredient list on screen
- Instagram collaborations with French amateur sport practitioners and local gym coaches showing the snack in their gym bag, with clear partnership labelling
- Counter display and sampling days in sport and organic shops, with a simple card showing the six ingredients
- Own online shop discovery pack with the three flavours and an Instagram-to-shop link, plus a short ingredient-list explainer page
- Instagram Stories poll asking the community to react on flavours, including early feedback on the upcoming Herbes de Provence flavour

**Visual direction.** Clean, energetic and direct. A bright white or warm off-white base with bold colour blocks per flavour: sea-blue for Sea Salt, herb green for Rosemary, and a warm paprika red for Smoked Paprika. Close-up macro photography of the crisp's crunch and texture, plus real training-bag and locker-room moments, with natural light and no overly muscular stereotypes. Typography is bold, sporty sans-serif with large '15 g' protein callouts. Ingredient list shown openly as a graphic element (six icons). Nutri-Score A logo placed clearly on Sea Salt and Rosemary materials only. Packaging cues: matte, paper-like compostable pack with a large flavour name, a clear 'végan' and 'sans gluten' marker, and a window-style crisp illustration. Gym assets are high-contrast and large-format, while supermarket assets are simple, with a strong shelf-stopping colour band.
