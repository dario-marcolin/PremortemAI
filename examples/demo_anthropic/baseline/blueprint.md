# Launch Plan: Proteo Crisps in France (Verdo Foods)

## 1. Executive summary

Verdo Foods plans to launch Proteo Crisps in France in March 2026, starting in the Paris, Lyon and Lille regions [3]. The product is an oven-baked chickpea and pea-protein crisp with 15 g of protein per 40 g bag, six ingredients and Nutri-Score A on two of three flavours [2]. It enters a protein snack category worth 480 million euros, growing 11% per year, where 27% of French adults bought a protein snack in the last three months [4].

**Recommendation:**
- **Do not target vegans first.** They show the highest purchase intent (72%) but number only 1.3 million adults, 2.5% of the population [5]. That is too small to deliver the board goals of a 2% share and 1,500 points of sale [3].
- **Primary target: health-conscious office workers** (9.0 million adults, 38% intent) [5]. They buy mainly in supermarkets, which carry 68% of category sales [4], and they respond to Nutri-Score and "everyday snack" messages, which fits the company mission [1].
- **Secondary target: weekly sport practitioners** (17.5 million, 41% intent) [5], reached through gyms and sport/organic shops. Vegans serve as an early community and credibility base, not the core target.
- **Positioning:** "the crisp you don't have to feel guilty about", meaning crisp first, protein second. Texture was the most appreciated attribute in Italian blind tests, and 47% preferred Proteo to leading potato crisps, with a further 21% tie [2].
- **Campaign:** "Craquez malin", built around the 4 pm office snack moment, with Nutri-Score A and 15 g of protein as proof points.
- **Key risks:** no price is defined and no competitor price comparison exists [6]. The French goals look internally inconsistent (see section 5). The taste test was run only in Italy [2].

## 3. Market and competitors

**Market overview**
- The French savoury snack market was 2.9 billion euros in 2024. The protein snack category was 480 million euros, growing 11% per year from 2021 to 2024. Plant-based products are 18% of the category [4].
- Plant-based protein snacks are therefore about 86 M€ (own calculation from [4]). This is the pool Proteo directly competes in.
- Channel split of protein snack sales: supermarkets and hypermarkets 68%, specialised sport and organic shops 17%, online 9%, gyms and vending 6% [4].
- French consumers know Nutri-Score, which was created in France [3]. This supports a shelf-level argument for Proteo's A rating on two flavours [2][3].

**Main competitors**

| Competitor | Positioning (per company document) | Price level |
|---|---|---|
| Barebells | Protein bars and snacks, strong in gyms and supermarkets, sport-oriented image [6] | Not documented; to be audited |
| Gerble | French health and wellness food brand, widely available in supermarkets, some protein products [6] | Not documented; to be audited |
| Supermarket private labels | Entered protein snacks in 2023 with lower prices [6] | Lower than branded products [6] |
| Conventional potato crisps (reference for taste) | Mass-market indulgence. Proteo beat the leading brand in Italian blind tests [2] | Not documented |

No detailed price comparison exists yet [6]. Completing a shelf price audit before the distributor negotiations close is a priority.

**Customer needs** (inferred from the document and our analysis)
- Protein without sacrificing taste and crunch. Texture was the most appreciated attribute in tests, while a quarter found Smoked Paprika too strong [2].
- Short, clean ingredient lists and a good Nutri-Score [2][3].
- Everyday availability in the supermarket, where 68% of category sales happen [4].
- Sustainable packaging [1][2].

## 4. Segments

Group sizes refer to adults aged 18 to 64 (about 51 million) [5]. "Estimated intenders" is size × purchase intent (own calculation from [5]). The groups overlap, so the figures must not be added together [5].

| Segment | Size (adults) | Share of adults | Purchase intent | Estimated intenders | Main needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vegans and strict vegetarians | 1.3 M | 2.5% | 72% | ~0.9 M | Plant-based credibility, certified vegan, protein. Very active on social media [5]. |
| Weekly sport practitioners | 17.5 M | 34% | 41% | ~7.2 M | Protein, convenient post- or pre-workout snack. Sport-oriented competitors are strong here [5][6]. |
| Health-conscious office workers | 9.0 M | 18% | 38% | ~3.4 M | Healthier everyday snack, clean label, Nutri-Score, taste (inferred). |
| Parents of school-age children | 7.1 M | 14% | 22% | ~1.6 M | Healthy snacks for the family (inferred). Lowest intent. Price sensitivity and strong flavours are likely barriers (inferred). |

**Reading the table**
- Vegans are the most enthusiastic group but the smallest, with fewer than one million likely buyers.
- Sport practitioners are the largest pool of likely buyers, but they are the segment where competitors are most entrenched [6].
- Office workers combine meaningful scale with high intent and a mainstream, supermarket-driven purchase pattern.
- Parents are a possible later extension, not a launch target.

## 5. Strategy

### Target segment
- **Primary: health-conscious office workers**, 9.0 M adults with 38% intent [5].
- **Secondary: weekly sport practitioners**, reached through gyms and sport shops [4][5]. Many are also office workers [5], so the two segments share messaging.
- **Community seeding only: vegans and vegetarians**, who act as early advocates on social media [5].
- **Deferred: parents of school-age children** [5].

### Rationale
1. **Scale versus the goals.** A 2% share of the 480 M€ category is about 9.6 M€ at 2024 size (own calculation from [3][4]). If growth continued at 11% per year, it would be roughly 13–15 M€ of retail value by early 2028 (illustrative projection). Vegans alone, at about 0.9 M likely buyers, are too narrow a base [5].
2. **Mission fit.** The company wants plant-based protein to be "an everyday snack, not a niche product for athletes or vegans" [1]. Targeting vegans first would reinforce the niche image the company wants to escape.
3. **Channel fit.** Supermarkets carry 68% of category sales [4], and the national distributor plan targets them [7]. Office workers buy there. Gyms and vending are only 6% of sales, and sport/organic shops 17% [4].
4. **Competitive space.** Barebells owns a sport-oriented image in gyms and supermarkets [6]. Leading with sport would put Proteo into direct confrontation. A "smart everyday snack" position is less crowded (to be validated).
5. **Social media enthusiasm is not purchase volume.** The founders' instinct to focus on vegans is based on enthusiasm and social activity [5], not on market size. Vegans should be used as a low-cost amplifier.

### Positioning
*For health-conscious people who want to snack better without giving up crunch, Proteo Crisps are the protein crisps that actually taste like crisps.*
- **Crisp first, protein second.** Italian blind tests: 47% preferred Proteo, 21% tie, 32% preferred the conventional potato crisp. Texture scored highest [2].
- The claim must be validated with a French consumer test before it is used in advertising, because the data comes from Italy and from company-run research [2].

### Value proposition
- **15 g of protein per 40 g bag** [2].
- **Nutri-Score A** (Sea Salt, Rosemary) and a six-ingredient list with no palm oil or additives [2].
- **Crunchy, tasty texture** [2].
- **Vegan, gluten-free, compostable packaging** [2].
- The "30% more protein" comparison is against Italian plant-based competitors [2]. Do not use it in France until checked against French competitors.

### Pricing direction
- No price exists in the document, and no French price comparison has been done [6]. The following is a direction, not a number.
- Price **above private labels** (which compete on low price [6]) and **at or close to the mainstream branded protein snack price**, on a per-100 g basis. This is a premium-accessible position that supports the "everyday" mission.
- Avoid a price premium so high that it recreates the niche image.
- Define the price after a shelf audit of Barebells, Gerble and private labels. Check the margin after distributor and retailer cuts against the break-even goal in year three [3].
- Consider a launch promotion in the first weeks, with trial packs for gyms and offices.

### Channels
| Channel | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Supermarkets and hypermarkets | Volume engine (68% of category sales [4]) | Through the national distributor [7]. Aim for the protein/healthy snack shelf and a secondary placement in the crisps aisle. |
| Sport and organic shops | Credibility and early adopters (17% [4]) | Direct sales in Paris, Lyon, Lille [7]. |
| Gyms and vending | Trial and secondary segment (6% [4]) | Direct sales [7]. Use for sampling. |
| Online | Reach beyond launch regions, data on French consumers (9% [4]) | Translated shop shipping from launch day [7]. |
| Workplace | Sampling to the primary target | Office canteens, coworking spaces and corporate wellness (our recommendation). |

### Goal consistency check (own calculation)
- **Share vs turnover.** A 2% share is about 9.6 M€ of retail value at 2024 category size [3][4]. The goal of 25% of company turnover from France by end 2027 equals roughly 1.4 M€ if Italian sales stay at 4.2 M€ [1][3]. The two goals imply very different French business sizes unless Italy grows strongly. Management should reconcile them.
- **Points of sale.** 1,500 points of sale in year one is about two-thirds of the 2,300 points of sale built in Italy [1][3]. This depends heavily on the distributor and the supermarket listing calendar.
- **Break-even in year three** needs the price, trade terms and marketing budget, none of which are in the document.

### Phasing
- **Q4 2025 – Q1 2026:** price audit, French consumer test, distributor decision, French pack with front-of-pack Nutri-Score [7][8].
- **March 2026:** launch in Paris, Lyon, Lille regions across supermarkets, gyms, organic shops and online [3][7].
- **Months 3–9:** adjust by sell-out data, introduce Herbes de Provence [2], extend to further regions.
- **Months 9–24:** national roll-out toward 1,500+ points of sale and the 2% share goal [3].

## 6. Campaign

### Concept
**"Le 16 h qui a du répondant."** The campaign is built on the afternoon snack slump at the office. Instead of the usual guilty crisp, a crunchy crisp with 15 g of protein [2]. The tone is light, a little ironic, never preachy, and the focus is on crunch, with protein as the smart reason to say yes. Vegan and sustainable credentials are supporting facts.

### Tagline (French)
**"Craquez malin."**
This plays on *craquer* (give in to a craving) and *malin* (smart), so the same snack satisfies the craving and is a sensible choice.

### Key messages per channel

| Channel | Message |
|---|---|
| Supermarket shelf and in-store | "15 g de protéines. Nutri-Score A. 100 % croquant." Front-of-pack Nutri-Score is shown on the French pack [7]. Shelf strips highlight protein per bag. |
| Gyms and sport shops | "Votre snack d'après-séance qui croque vraiment." Sampling, with protein as the headline [5]. |
| Organic shops | "Six ingrédients, zéro huile de palme, emballage compostable." [2] |
| Online shop and social media | Short humorous videos of the 16 h office craving. Recipes and "protein snack" content. Engage the vegan and vegetarian community as ambassadors [5]. |
| Workplace sampling | "Pause croquante, sans culpabilité." Sample boxes in offices and coworking spaces (recommendation). |
| PR and trade | "Un fabricant italien lance en France le chips protéiné qui a du goût." Emphasis on the Nutri-Score A rating and on the French-specific flavour [2][3]. |

### Content ideas
- **"Le test à l'aveugle":** a French blind test video, only to be run once real French results are available, since the Italian result cannot be quoted as a French fact [2].
- **"Les 16 h":** a series of short sketches showing office snack temptations and Proteo as the clever alternative.
- **Herbes de Provence launch:** a "created for France" story with local influencers and food creators [2].
- **Nutri-Score explained:** simple graphics comparing Nutri-Score and protein per bag, but only after checking that comparisons are fair and legally compliant [3].
- **Vegan and vegetarian ambassador programme:** product seeding and user-generated content [5].
- **Office sampling tour** in Paris, Lyon, Lille, followed by a "best snack drawer" competition.
- **Compostable pack story:** how to dispose of the pack, tied to the 2024 packaging introduction [2].

### Visual direction
- Bright, warm colours (golden yellow, chickpea beige, a bold accent for each flavour).
- Close-up macro photography emphasising crunch, such as a bite, crumbs and the sound.
- Real office and daily-life settings rather than gym-only imagery, to avoid copying the sport-oriented image of competitors [6].
- Large, clear Nutri-Score and "15 g de protéines" badges on the pack and in all advertising [2][7].
- Lead with Sea Salt and Rosemary (Nutri-Score A), since a quarter of Italian testers found Smoked Paprika too strong [2].
- Natural materials and simple typography that echo the short ingredient list and compostable packaging [2].

### KPIs to track
- Points of sale listed against the 1,500 target [3].
- Value share of the protein snack category against the 2% target [3].
- Rate of sale per point of sale.
- Repeat purchase, trial through sampling, and online conversion.
- Brand awareness among office workers in the three launch regions.
